How do AI writing tools actually work with WordPress?

This is the question that decides which tool to buy, and most articles skip it. There are three ways an AI writing tool touches WordPress, and they're very different in practice:

WorkflowHow It WorksSuited ForExample
Native pluginInstall the tool's plugin on your site, generate in the app, publish with one clickSites publishing several posts a weekWritesonic
Browser extensionThe tool runs in your browser and works inside the WordPress editorQuick drafts and in-editor editingRytr, Grammarly
Copy-pasteGenerate in the tool, paste into the block editor, format manuallyEveryone — especially free tiersClaude, ChatGPT

Over the 30-day test, the copy-paste workflow was the most common and the most reliable — it works with every tool, every theme, and every version of WordPress. The native plugin saves the most time but exists in only a few tools. Browser extensions sit in the middle: convenient for editing, not for generating a full article from scratch.

Which AI writing tool has a real WordPress plugin?

Fewer than you'd think. Native WordPress plugins are rarer than the marketing pages suggest.

Writesonic is the standout: its official free plugin connects your site, lets you publish AI-generated articles (title, meta description, slug, tags, images) directly to WordPress, and can update existing posts. It's a genuine "generate and publish" workflow. Jasper has a basic WordPress integration, but it's nowhere near as deep — in testing it covered the draft step, not the publishing step.

Everything else relies on the browser or copy-paste. Rytr and Grammarly work through browser extensions inside the editor. Claude and ChatGPT have no WordPress connection at all.

Does Grammarly work inside WordPress?

Yes — but it's not a plugin you install on your server. Grammarly runs as a browser extension and activates inside the WordPress block editor, underlining grammar, clarity, and tone issues as you type or paste. For a tool that generates rough AI drafts, that's exactly where you want the editor to sit: on top of the finished text, not inside the generator.

The free tier catches the basics (spelling and obvious grammar). Premium at $12/mo (annual) adds clarity rewrites and tone adjustments, which is where AI drafts actually need help — AI text tends to be grammatically correct but wordy and flat. The pair I kept coming back to in testing was AI writes → Grammarly polishes → publish.

Are free tools like Claude or ChatGPT enough for WordPress?

For occasional posts, yes. Claude's free tier produced the most natural long-form drafts in my 30-day test, and ChatGPT's free tier handles quick outlines and rewrites well. Neither connects to WordPress, so you copy the output into the block editor and format it manually.

The tradeoff is time, not quality. When you paste a full article, you still rebuild headings, lists, images, and internal links by hand. If you publish a few posts a month, that's fine. If you publish every day, the minutes add up — and that's exactly the case where Jasper's brand voice and SEO workflow earn their $49/mo for teams publishing at scale.

How do you optimize AI content for WordPress SEO?

WordPress already ships the basics — clean URLs, fast loading, XML sitemaps. The plugins that matter for ranking (Yoast SEO or RankMath) score your content for the target keyword. In testing, the winning routine was:

  • Generate the article with the keyword in the title, first paragraph, and an H2.
  • Paste it into WordPress, then run Yoast or RankMath's readability and SEO checks.
  • Rewrite any sentence flagged as wordy — this is where Grammarly earns its place.
  • Add a real meta description, alt text on images, and 2-3 internal links to your own posts.

Tools with a strong SEO workflow, like Jasper's Surfer integration, front-load this work: the draft arrives closer to publish-ready, and your WordPress SEO plugin becomes a sanity check instead of a rewrite. No tool replaces the human pass on facts and claims — AI drafts still need that.
