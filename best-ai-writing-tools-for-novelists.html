The Honest Truth About AI + Fiction

After 30 days of testing, here's the reality: AI is a brilliant assistant and a mediocre author.

It shines at the planning — worldbuilding, character profiles, chapter outlines, plot structure. And it's excellent at dialogue and short scenes with the right prompts.

But full-chapter AI prose reads flat after a while. The writers getting the most value use AI to scaffold, then write or heavily rewrite the prose themselves. The best results came from feeding the AI your own writing as a voice reference.

How to Use AI for Your Novel (Workflow)

  1. Worldbuild with AI: Generate premises, magic systems, factions, and lore
  2. Build characters: Core desire, fatal flaw, backstory, voice
  3. Outline scenes: Entering emotion, inciting moment, turning point, exit hook
  4. Draft with AI as scaffolding: Generate a rough scene, then rewrite it in your voice
  5. Edit aggressively: The final voice must be yours — AI output is a starting point, not the finish

Voice — The Hardest Part

The #1 complaint writers have is "AI prose all sounds the same." Our fix: paste 2-3 paragraphs of your own writing into the prompt as a style reference. It outperforms describing your tone with adjectives. Combined with heavy editing, this keeps your voice intact.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can AI help write a novel?

Yes — for planning, worldbuilding, outlining, and dialogue. Most writers use it for scaffolding, then write or rewrite the prose themselves.

What is the best AI for fiction writing?

Claude for the most natural prose; ChatGPT for versatility.

Is it ethical to use AI to write a novel?

Check publisher policies — many require disclosure or prohibit AI content. Self-publishing gives more freedom.

How do I use AI without losing my voice?

Feed AI your own writing samples, use it for scaffolding, and edit every output.
