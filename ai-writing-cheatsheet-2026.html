The point of a cheatsheet is to stop overthinking. Most people lose money on AI writing tools not because the tools are bad, but because they bought the wrong one for the job, or they shipped raw output without editing. This one page fixes both.

The 30-second buy filter

Before paying for anything, answer five questions. If you cannot answer them, you are not ready to buy — and no tool will save you.

  • What exactly will I write? Short repeated assets and long-form articles need different tools.
  • What is my monthly volume? A few posts a week favours a cheap flat plan; thousands of words daily favours unlimited.
  • Do I need SEO scoring? If ranking is the goal, pick a tool with native scoring. If not, don't pay for it.
  • Is brand voice consistency required? Teams and agencies need voice memory; solo writers usually don't.
  • What is my real budget? The cheapest dedicated writer is $7.50/month. Most needs are covered well below $50.

The prompt formula that actually works

Vague prompts produce vague drafts. Use this five-part skeleton every time:

Role: you are a [specific role]
Task: write [format] for [audience]
Context: [what they know / don't know]
Constraints: [tone, length, must-avoid]
Format: [headings / bullets / table]

The "Constraints" line is what separates usable output from generic sludge. Always state what the tool must not do.

The 5 mistakes that get AI writing flagged

  • Publishing with no human edit — factual errors survive untouched.
  • Keyword stuffing because "SEO" was mentioned in the prompt.
  • Letting the tool invent stats, prices, or quotes.
  • Copy-pasting the same draft to ten pages (duplicate content).
  • Trusting a "ranking guarantee" — no writing tool can promise that.
