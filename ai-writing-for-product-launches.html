The short answer: A product launch needs seven pieces of copy — landing page, launch email, follow-up emails, social posts, FAQ, and product description — and they need to sound like one company. Draft all seven from a single brief with AI, in one sitting, then edit for consistency. The brief, not the tool, is what makes a launch sound unified.

The 7 pieces every launch needs

  1. Landing page — the offer, the action, the objection. See how to write one with AI.
  2. Launch email — announce, with a clear reason to act now.
  3. Follow-up sequence — 2-3 emails for non-openers, objectors, and the on-the-fencers.
  4. Social posts — 3-5 posts across your channels, one message each.
  5. FAQ — the 8-10 questions people actually ask (support can supply these).
  6. Product description — the feature/benefit copy that lives on the product page.
  7. Release/privacy notes — the boilerplate that's easy to forget until it's urgent.

One brief, all seven

Write one brief and feed it to every piece:

Product: [what it is, the price]. Audience: [who it's for, what they've tried]. The one action: [buy / sign up]. The objection to handle: [the top reason they'd hesitate]. Voice: [plain-spoken, no hype].

Draft all seven in one session. That's the trick — the same brief flowing into every touchpoint is what produces a unified voice, which is the difference between a launch and a collection of pages. For the email side specifically, the email with AI workflow follows the same brief-first rule.

What each piece needs from you

  • Landing page: rewrite the first paragraph by hand; enforce one CTA.
  • Emails: cut every robot line; add one specific detail per email.
  • Social: shrink to platform-native length; keep the one message.
  • FAQ: only real questions — swap in the ones support actually gets.
  • Product description: benefits over features, concrete numbers, no superlatives.
