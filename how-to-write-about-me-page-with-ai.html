Nobody struggles to write a blog post like they struggle to write an About page. The post has a topic; the About page has you — and writing about yourself in a way strangers trust is genuinely hard. This is exactly the problem AI is good at and bad at at the same time: it removes the blank page, but it can't supply the specific, human details that make the page work. Here's the process we recommend, the three questions that do the heavy lifting, and what to never let AI write.

1. Why the About Page Is Harder Than It Looks

An About page does two jobs at once. It's a pitch — here's what I do and why you should care — and it's a proof of trust — here's why I'm credible. Most people nail the pitch and stall on the trust part, which is why so many pages end at "I'm a writer with a passion for words." That sentence means nothing, and an AI will happily produce more sentences just like it.

The fix is to flip the order: gather the trust material first, then let AI shape it. If you're a freelancer, our AI writing tools for freelancers guide covers the same principle for the rest of your client-facing pages.

2. The 3 Questions Only You Can Answer

Before any AI touches the page, answer these three questions in writing. They're the load-bearing parts of your About page, and no model can invent them for you.

  • Why did you start? The specific moment or problem that got you here — not a mission statement.
  • How are you different? The thing you do, the way you do it, or the person you serve that your competitors don't match.
  • What's one real number? Years, clients, readers, projects, a measurable result. Specific beats impressive every time.

Answer each in three sentences or fewer. You now have the entire substance of the page — the AI's job is just to arrange it.

3. Use AI for the Frame, Not the Story

With your three answers written, prompt the AI with the exact material and ask for structure, not invention: "Here are my answers. Draft a short About page: an opening hook, a paragraph on what I do, a paragraph on my difference, and one result. Don't add facts." Because the model is arranging your words rather than generating from scratch, the output tends to stay close to your voice — and the lies stay out.

For a first pass, the free tiers of ChatGPT or Claude are more than enough. Both scored 8/10 and 9/10 on short-form in our 30-day benchmark, and an About page is short-form work by definition. You can try ChatGPT or try Claude directly — the free tiers are all you need.

4. What to Never Let AI Write

Some parts of an About page are off-limits for a model, no matter how good the draft quality. Keep these human and exact:

  • Your experience, credentials, and employment history
  • Claims about results, client counts, or revenue
  • Names, dates, and any verifiable facts
  • Your reasons for starting — the AI didn't live them

If a claim would embarrass you when a reader fact-checks it, it doesn't belong in an AI draft at all. Write those lines yourself, word by word.

5. Keep It Short and Specific

Visitors decide in seconds whether to trust you, so the page should be short enough to read in one breath: an opening line, two tight paragraphs, one number, and a call to action. If a sentence could describe any freelancer in your field, cut it. If it could only describe you, keep it.

This is the same discipline we apply to every page on this site — our how we test page exists because specifics beat claims. Apply the standard to yourself: one true, specific thing is worth more than a paragraph of generic enthusiasm.

6. The Edit Pass That Makes It Sound Like You

Take the AI draft and do one pass with your three answers open next to it. Restore any specific detail the model flattened into a general statement. Shorten sentences, remove formal transitions, and make sure the opening line sounds like you speaking, not a website announcing you. Read it out loud — if a sentence is awkward to say, it's awkward to read.

Our guide on making AI writing sound human lists the exact patterns to remove. The goal isn't to hide that you used AI; it's to make the page true, which is a higher standard than sounding un-AI.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use AI to write my About Me page?

Yes — use it for structure and a first draft, then answer the three questions only you know: why you started, what makes you different, and one real number. That personal layer is what makes the page sound like you.

What should I never let AI write on an About page?

Your experience, credentials, and claims about results. AI doesn't know what you actually did, so anything it invents is a credibility and trust risk. Write those facts yourself, verify every date and number, and keep them exact — no filler and no inflation.

How long should an About Me page be?

Short and specific. A few tight paragraphs — your story, your difference, one concrete result, and a call to action — beat a long bio. Most visitors decide in seconds whether to trust you, so lead with your strongest proof.

What's the best AI tool for writing an About page?

ChatGPT and Claude free tiers are enough — both scored 8/10 and 9/10 on short-form writing, which is exactly what an About page needs. Paying for a tool only helps if you want a consistent brand voice across many pages.
