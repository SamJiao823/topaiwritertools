Every affiliate site faces the same question: how much AI is too much? We run a testing-first site, and the honest answer is that AI is a drafting tool, not a substitute for experience. This guide is the exact workflow we use — AI writes the structure and the first pass, and your testing data is what makes the review worth ranking.

1. The One Rule That Keeps AI Reviews Honest

AI can generate a plausible-looking review in ten seconds. The moment that matters is the next line: have you used the product? If you haven't, an AI review is a lie with good grammar, and Google's helpful-content guidance increasingly buries generic roundups that don't reflect first-hand experience. We know from running this site for months — our benchmark page, built on real 30-day testing, is the page that actually performs.

The workflow below assumes you have real usage. If you don't, the honest play is a "hands-off comparison" clearly labeled, or an aggregate of other people's documented tests — not a fake first-person review.

2. Step 1 — Draft With Structure, Not Fluff

Feed the AI a tight outline, not a blank page. For an affiliate review, the sections readers actually want are: what it costs, what it's for, what's good, what's bad, who it's for, and one clear verdict. Ask the AI to fill each section from the bullet points you provide — not to invent new claims.

If you want the AI to handle the whole first pass, Writesonic is our pick: $20/mo, 8/10 short-form, 7/10 long-form, native SEO tools (from our 30-day benchmark). Its SEO module suggests headings and keywords while you draft, which saves the editing pass. On a budget, Rytr at $7.50/mo handles the short sections fine but averages ~22 minutes of editing per piece — acceptable for a snippet, painful for a 2,000-word review.

3. Step 2 — Feed It Your Data, Not Its Opinions

This is the difference between an AI review and an affiliate review written with AI. Every number in the review should be your number: price you paid, time you tested, scores you assigned, screenshots you took. Prompt the AI like this:

"Here are my test notes: [paste]. Write a 1,200-word review with sections for pricing, pros, cons, and who it's for. Do not add facts that aren't in my notes."

Then read the output against your notes line by line. If the AI added a claim, cut it or verify it. On this site we keep a public how we test page precisely so readers can audit what we claim.

4. Step 3 — Disclose Everything

Disclosure isn't just an FTC requirement; it's the trust asset. Three things, in order: a visible disclosure near the top of the page (we use one right under the hero), rel="nofollow sponsored" on affiliate links, and a plain-language note in the review itself ("links to X are affiliate links"). Our affiliate disclosure page spells out the policy we apply site-wide.

A quick SEO note: Google treats affiliate-heavy content under its site reputation abuse and helpful content guidance. Well-disclosed, genuinely useful reviews are exactly what that guidance wants.

5. Step 4 — Sound Like a Human, Not a Template

AI drafts carry tells: perfectly balanced sentences, "moreover" paragraphs, no awkward details. Kill the tells in one editing pass. Concretely: shorten sentences, add one specific (true) detail per section, break the rhythm, use "I" honestly. For the mechanics of that pass, our guide on making AI writing sound human goes deep on the exact patterns to remove.

And keep the honest-voice rules from our is using AI writing cheating post in mind — disclosing the AI assistance is itself a trust signal.

6. Step 5 — Write for the Decision, Not the Word Count

The best affiliate reviews answer one question clearly: should this reader buy? Structure the verdict so a skimmer can get the answer in ten seconds — a score, a one-line recommendation, and a "who should buy" box. Then let the body support that verdict with data. This mirrors how our ecommerce copy pack pages are built: verdict first, detail second.

7. Step 6 — The Editing Checklist

  • Claims: every fact traceable to a test note or official source.
  • Disclosure: visible at top + rel="nofollow sponsored" on affiliate links.
  • Balance: at least one genuine limitation per tool. No tool is "the best" — we never say that here.
  • Human pass: two concrete details per section that only a user would know.
  • Verdict: readable in 10 seconds.

When in doubt, remember the site's rule: don't say any tool is the best. Say what it's best for, back it with data, and let readers decide. That's what makes an affiliate review earn clicks, commissions, and — over time — a brand people trust.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use AI to write affiliate reviews?

Yes, if you have hands-on experience and you disclose it. AI drafts the structure and language; your real testing data and opinions are what make it trustworthy.

Will Google rank AI-written affiliate reviews?

It ranks pages that help people, not pages that are or aren't AI-written. Real test data, honest limitations, and clear disclosure are what earn a spot.

How do I disclose affiliate links properly?

A clear disclosure near the top, rel="nofollow sponsored" on links, and a plain-language note. Being upfront keeps reader trust.

What's the best AI tool for writing reviews?

For structured review drafts, Writesonic at $20/mo is the best value (native SEO tools included). Rytr at $7.50/mo handles short snippets on a budget.
