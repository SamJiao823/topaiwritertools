The 30-second answer

Pick ChatGPT for writing. Pick Writesonic only if AI search visibility is a goal you measure. ChatGPT scored 8/10 on short-form and 8/10 on long-form in our 30-day blind test; Writesonic scored 8/10 and 7/10 — the same price band's writing, for $79 a month against ChatGPT's $0–20. What Writesonic has that ChatGPT does not is GEO tooling built into the drafting workflow, and that is the entire justification for the price. On writing alone, ChatGPT wins and it isn't close on cost.

Where these numbers come from

Every score here is ours. Seven tools ran through the same 30-day blind test and were scored on six weighted dimensions: Content Quality (25%), Features & Capabilities (20%), SEO & AI Search / GEO (15%), Ease of Use (15%), Value for Money (15%), and Support & Reliability (10%). Reviewers scoring drafts did not know which tool had produced which — in the blind round they could not reliably tell which tool drafted a given product description.

The full table is in our 2026 AI writing tools benchmark, and the method is documented on our How We Test page. Note that we publish the columns each tool loses. Writesonic loses four of six against a free product, which is worth knowing before you read its marketing.

Two facts that matter more than the feature list. First, there is no free plan and no free tier — unlike ChatGPT, which you can use indefinitely at $0 with the same 8/10 short-form score. Second, billing monthly costs about $20/month more than annual, so the discount is real but it also removes your exit if the workflow doesn't fit.

Which one writes better?

They tie on short-form and ChatGPT leads on long-form. Both scored 8/10 on product descriptions, emails, and captions; on long pieces ChatGPT held 8/10 while Writesonic slipped to 7/10, matching the degradation pattern we documented in why AI long-form fails.

That is a narrower gap than most comparisons claim, and it cuts both ways. If you were expecting $79 to buy visibly better prose, it doesn't. If you assumed a template-driven tool writes worse, the short-form tie says otherwise — Writesonic's output is structurally clean and needs little reorganizing, which is exactly why its edit time is competitive.

The GEO question: does it justify the price?

This is the only honest reason to choose Writesonic, so it deserves a straight answer. GEO tooling inside the drafting workflow means keyword research, SERP context, and on-page optimization happen while the article is being written rather than in a separate pass. If you publish SEO content on a schedule and ranking in AI-generated answers is a metric you actually track, that consolidation is worth real money — it removes a tool and a step from every piece.

If you do not track any of that, the feature set is inert. This is the most common bad purchase in the category: paying a GEO premium for content that is judged on readership, not on citations.

There is no break-even point here. Unlike the budget-tier comparisons, where a cheap tool eventually becomes the expensive one, Writesonic stays the more expensive option at every volume.

Which is fine — as long as you are buying it for the one thing that isn't on this table. The GEO tooling has to be worth roughly $66 a month to you at four pieces. If it is, the arithmetic is irrelevant. If you can't finish that sentence, it isn't.

Which one to pick

Three conditions. Find yours.

  • You need writing, and only writing. ChatGPT, at the free tier if you write occasionally and Plus if you write daily. Our breakdown of whether ChatGPT Plus is worth paying for covers where the free tier stops being enough.
  • You publish SEO content weekly and track AI-answer visibility. Writesonic, on annual billing, with an afternoon set aside to learn the workflow. This is the case the product was built for and the only one where $79 makes sense.
  • You want the best writing per dollar. Neither of the above. Claude at $20 scored 9/10 on both short and long-form and edited fastest in our test — see our Claude Pro review.

When neither is the right answer

Three cases where this comparison is the wrong question.

You are paying per seat for a team of writers. Neither tool solves brand consistency, which is the one problem a team actually has. Without an answer for that, you are buying each writer a faster blank page. That is a different purchase, and it is the only one Jasper is genuinely built for.

You want AI-answer visibility but don't produce the pages. If freelancers or an agency writes your content, the GEO tool belongs in their stack, not your subscription. Paying $79/month to review other people's drafts is the most avoidable purchase on this page.

Your pages are published and simply not ranking. Then the constraint is authority or search intent, not drafting speed — and a GEO workflow will produce better-optimized pages that still don't rank. In our testing the human editor was the quality bottleneck every time; no subscription fixes a strategy problem, it just makes the wrong pages faster to build.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is ChatGPT or Writesonic better?

For writing, ChatGPT — it tied Writesonic at 8/10 on short-form, leads on long-form 8/10 to 7/10, and costs $0 to $20 instead of $79. Writesonic is better only at SEO and GEO features, where ChatGPT has nothing built in.

Is Writesonic worth paying for over ChatGPT?

Only if AI search visibility is a goal you measure and act on. The $79/month Starter plan bundles keyword research, SEO scoring, and GEO tracking into the drafting workflow, which ChatGPT does not do at any price. For pure writing, ChatGPT is both cheaper and ahead.

Does Writesonic have a free plan?

No. There is no free plan and no free tier in 2026, and the old $20 writing plan has been retired. Writesonic starts at the $79/month Starter tier. ChatGPT, by contrast, can be used indefinitely at $0.

Can I use both together?

Some teams do, and it is a defensible split if you are already paying for Writesonic: draft in ChatGPT for the 8/10 short-form output, then run the piece through Writesonic's optimization and GEO tracking. What you should not do is pay for both on the theory that they overlap — on short-form they score identically.

Is Writesonic better than ChatGPT for SEO content?

Yes, and this is the one clear win. Writesonic is the only tool in our benchmark with GEO built natively into the drafting workflow rather than added on, and it scores highest on our SEO and AI Search dimension. Its long-form score of 7/10 means it is strongest on the 1,500-word commercial article rather than very long pieces.

Which is cheaper for a solo writer?

ChatGPT, at every volume. At four pieces a month ChatGPT Plus costs about $53 in subscription plus editing time against Writesonic's $119, and because Writesonic is also slower to edit, the gap never closes as volume rises. There is no break-even point between these two.

Should I buy Writesonic if I only write short-form?

No. Short-form is where the two tie at 8/10, so you would be paying $59 a month more for output of the same measured quality. If your work is emails, captions, and product copy, a free or $20 generalist tool covers it.
