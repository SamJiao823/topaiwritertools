What a Local Business Actually Needs to Write

Most local content fits three buckets, and none of them are long:

  • Google Business Profile posts — offers, updates, and events, a few sentences each
  • A simple blog — one local topic a week, five to eight paragraphs
  • Service pages — the pages that say what you do, where, and for how much

Because the volume is high and the length is short, a cheap dedicated writer covers most of it. That's the opposite of a national content strategy, and it changes which tool is best for you. Our guide to AI writing tools for small business covers the same ground from the owner's side.

Short, Local, Consistent

Three words rule local content: short, local, consistent. A Google post that says "Spring tire check at our Main Street shop — free pressure check this week" beats a generic two-paragraph advertisement. Consistency matters more than polish, because Google rewards a profile that posts regularly.

That combination — short + local + regular — is exactly what our benchmark found budget tools handle well. You don't need a $49 tool for a four-sentence post; you need a cheap one you'll actually use.

Rytr — Best Budget for Google Business Posts and Offers

Rytr is the cheapest dedicated AI writer we tested at $7.50/month with unlimited characters, and it's best value when most of your output is short. It scores 7/10 on short-form — plenty for offers, updates, and quick blurbs. Our Rytr review and the how to use Rytr guide cover the short-copy workflow.

The honest caveat is editing time: about 22 minutes per piece, the highest on the board. For a four-sentence Google post that's heavy — which is why we suggest batching your posts and using Rytr for a whole month of content in one sitting. It's at rytr.me.

Writesonic — Best for Service Pages and Blog Posts That Rank

The moment you publish a blog or a service page, you want local keywords handled properly, and Writesonic is the only budget tool with native SEO tools. At $20/month it scored 8/10 short-form and 7/10 long-form, with about 12 minutes of editing — see the Writesonic review for the details.

It's best value for a local business that's ready to treat its blog as a channel: one local topic a week, optimized for "near me" searches, published consistently. Check writesonic.com for current pricing. Our guide to writing a blog post with AI walks through that exact process.

Claude and ChatGPT — Best Free Ways to Start

If you want to spend nothing while you figure out what works, ChatGPT (8/10 short-form) and Claude (9/10 short-form) draft Google posts and blog sections at $0-20/month. Paste your town, your services, and your hours into the prompt, and you'll get a usable first draft in about 10 minutes of editing.

The trade-off is workflow: free tools don't remember your business, so you re-paste context every time. That's fine for a weekly post and tedious for daily posting. Start free, and upgrade when the routine becomes real.

Grammarly — the Proofread That Keeps You Professional

A typo in a Google Business post is the local equivalent of a typo in a storefront sign. Grammarly at $30/month catches grammar and tone slips before you hit publish — our Grammarly review breaks down the free and paid tiers. It's an editor, not a generator, so pair it with one of the drafting tools above.

For a local business, Grammarly is usually the second purchase after the writer itself — it's the cheapest way to look professional in front of neighbors.

A Simple Weekly Local Content Routine

Here's a routine that matches how the tools actually test:

  1. Monday: one Google Business post — an offer or a local update, drafted in Rytr or ChatGPT
  2. Wednesday: one blog post or service page section, drafted in Writesonic or Claude
  3. Friday: proofread everything in Grammarly and schedule

Budget the editing honestly: about 22 minutes per piece with Rytr, 12 with Writesonic, 10 with ChatGPT. That's the real cost of local content — AI removes the typing, not the review. We say what each tool is best for, never that one tool is the best overall, because the right pick depends on how much of your output is short.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can AI write Google Business Profile posts?

Yes, and it's a good fit because those posts are short. Rytr at $7.50/month is best budget for quick offers and updates, while Claude and ChatGPT handle them free. Keep every post local and specific — mention your town, your services, and a clear call to action.

What's the cheapest AI writer for a small business?

Rytr is the cheapest dedicated AI writer we tested at $7.50/month with unlimited characters. It scores 7/10 on short-form, which is plenty for Google Business posts and simple blurbs. Claude and ChatGPT cost nothing, but you lose the dedicated writer workflow.

Do local businesses need a blog?

A simple blog helps service pages rank, but consistency matters more than length. Writesonic at $20/month is best value for blog posts that target local keywords because SEO tools are native. Claude and ChatGPT can draft too, and free works when you're starting out.

Can AI replace hiring a local copywriter?

For short, routine content, largely yes — offers, posts, and simple blogs. For high-stakes copy like your homepage or legal pages, keep a human. AI drafts save time, but a local voice and accurate details still come from you editing the result.
