The Short Answer

Rytr can write SEO content — but it's not the best tool for it. For short SEO pieces (meta descriptions, product descriptions, social captions) it's excellent value. For long-form articles, the quality drop past 1,000 words makes it a poor fit.

Rytr's SEO Features, Tested

  • Keyword generator: basic, no search volume data
  • SERP analyzer: shallow — no keyword difficulty
  • No content optimization scoring
  • No GEO/AI-search tracking
  • Plagiarism checker (Copyscape): useful, included in Saver plan

The Long-Form Problem

We attempted three 2,000+ word SEO articles with Rytr and abandoned all of them. Output became circular and repetitive past 1,000 words. For articles, you're better off with Writesonic ($20/mo) or Claude (free).

When Rytr IS Worth It for SEO

  • Meta descriptions & title tags
  • Product descriptions at scale
  • Social captions that support SEO
  • Email sequences to capture search traffic
  • Budget sites where cost matters more than depth
