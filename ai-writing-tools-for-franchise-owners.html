Franchise writing is ordinary small-business writing with an extra rule: it has to match a brand you do not own. Most generated copy drifts from approved phrasing without doing anything obviously wrong — the offer is slightly different, the claim slightly stronger, the wording slightly off-brand. Left unchecked over a year, twenty locations end up sounding like twenty companies.

Where AI clearly helps

  • Local marketing copy. Seasonal posts, in-store signage, localised versions of national campaigns. Same content adapted to your town, your hours, your stock.
  • Recruitment posts. The honest hardest part of the role, who it suits, how to apply. High turnover means writing this repeatedly.
  • Review responses. Thanking the positive ones and handling the negative ones without escalating. This is where most locations lose the most ground.
  • Customer replies to common questions. Opening hours, parking, allergens, wait times, gift cards. The same ten questions in every location.
  • Staff communications. Rota changes, procedure updates, reminders — clearer when consistently structured.
  • Reporting to head office. Monthly summaries with consistent headings, because someone reads twenty of them.
  • Local partnerships. Approach messages to nearby schools, clubs and employers, which few locations ever get round to writing.

Where it nearly got us in trouble

  • Off-brand claims. A generated offer or guarantee that the brand does not run. This is the most common and it is discovered by head office, not by you.
  • Pricing. Franchise pricing often has local variation rules and permitted ranges. A plausible-looking generated price can breach both.
  • Allergen and dietary statements. A vastly higher-stakes version of the same problem as a generated product description; a wrong sentence here has health consequences.
  • Employment language. Wording about pay, hours, contracts and dismissals. Generated HR text is confidently wrong often enough to be dangerous.
  • Replying defensively to reviews. Generated replies tend to argue, or to imply the customer is mistaken. That reply is public and permanent.
  • National campaign wording. Editing a supplied campaign for local use, then accidentally changing the offer.

When the national campaign does not fit your town

Head office supplies a campaign written for the average location, which fits nobody exactly. The temptation is to rewrite it so it suits your circumstances, and that is where messes start — usually when pricing, availability or a local event gets written into an approved offer. A safer approach: keep every sentence carrying the offer verbatim, and vary only the wrapper — why it matters here, who it suits locally, when to come in. If your location cannot run the offer as written, ask rather than adjusting it. The answer takes a day; explaining an unauthorised offer to a regional manager takes longer, and it is discovered at exactly the wrong moment.

Editing time, from our 30-day benchmark

Our 30-day test measured roughly 22 minutes of editing per 1,000 words on the low-cost dedicated writer against about 8 minutes on the general assistant — near $7 per piece of your time at $30 per hour. For a single site, the saving is concentrated in recruitment and review responses; across ten sites coordinated centrally, it is larger, because the templates are written once and reused everywhere.

A workflow that holds up

Put your brand guidelines and approved-phrasing list into the instruction and keep them there, every time — this is the one thing a model will not maintain across sessions. Build four or five local templates that only take variables you control: dates, location, stock, staff name. Generate national campaigns as near-verbatim edits rather than rewrites, changing only what you are permitted to change. Route prices, claims and employment wording past approval rather than past a draft. And when replying to a bad review, write the first sentence yourself — the acknowledgement is the part that decides whether the thread escalates.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use AI for franchise marketing?

For localised versions of approved messages, yes. Keep pricing, guarantees and special offers to what the brand permits, and paste your guidelines into every prompt.

How do I stop locations drifting off-brand?

Centralise the templates so locations fill variables rather than writing copy, and keep the approved-phrasing list in the standing instruction. Drift is cumulative and hard to spot from inside one location.

Should I generate responses to negative reviews?

Generate a structure, then write the acknowledgement yourself. Generated replies tend to argue or imply the customer is wrong, and that reply is permanent and public.

Is it safe for HR or employment wording?

No. Anything about pay, contracts, hours or dismissal needs approval from whoever owns that risk — generated HR language is confidently wrong often enough to matter.
