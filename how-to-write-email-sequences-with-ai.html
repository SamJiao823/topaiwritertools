Why sequences are a strong AI-writing investment

Email has the highest ROI of any channel, and sequences are the highest-leverage part of email. But a sequence is a series of short, formulaic messages — exactly what AI does well. Our 30-day test produced a full welcome flow in an afternoon that would have taken two days manually.

The sequence architecture

Before any prompt, define the sequence's job. We tested four types:

  • Welcome flow — 3-4 emails: prove value, tell the story, offer the first purchase.
  • Abandoned cart — 3 emails: reminder, social proof, incentive.
  • Follow-up — 2-3 touches after a no-reply.
  • Nurture — weekly value emails that build trust before selling.

The architecture comes first; the AI fills in the words. This is covered in depth in our email-marketing tool guide.

The prompt pattern that worked

One email per prompt, each with a single job. The template from our tests:

You are an email copywriter for [store/brand]. Write email #2 of a [type] sequence for [audience]. Its only job: [one outcome, e.g. 'get them to try the product']. Start with [specific hook], use [one piece of social proof], and end with one CTA: [action]. 90-120 words. Conversational, no jargon.

Giving each email one job was the single biggest quality lever — sequences drafted this way needed far less editing than 'write me a welcome sequence' outputs.

Where the tools differ

Short-form, so the price gap shrinks:

  • ChatGPT (free) — our pick for the bulk of sequence drafting. Good prose, free, unlimited-ish.
  • Rytr ($7.50) — email templates built in; output about 90% of Jasper's quality on short copy in testing.
  • Claude Pro ($20) — best tone control for brand-voice-heavy sequences.
  • Jasper ($39) — brand voice memory for teams; hard to justify for a solo store.

The editing pass that matters

Two specific edits made the AI drafts convert in our tests:

  1. Rewrite the first line. Models open with 'Hey there!' or 'We wanted to let you know'. Start with the specific hook from your brief instead.
  2. Add the proof. Models can't invent social proof. Paste in your real review, your real number, your real result. That's the difference between a sequence that gets deleted and one that gets clicked.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can AI write a whole email sequence?

Yes, with one email per prompt and each email given a single job. Our 30-day test produced a complete welcome flow in an afternoon. The editing pass — rewriting first lines and adding real proof — is what makes it convert.

How do I choose an AI tool for email sequences?

For short-form sequence drafting, ChatGPT's free tier is our pick. Rytr ($7.50) adds built-in email templates at about 90% of Jasper's short-copy quality. Claude Pro is strong for brand-voice-heavy sequences.

How many emails should a sequence have?

Welcome flows work best at 3-4 emails, abandoned cart at 3, follow-ups at 2-3. Keep each email to one job and one CTA — that's the structure that tested well.

Should I use AI for all my email copy?

Use it for the bulk drafting and the repetitive flows. Write the welcome email's opening and anything highly personal yourself. The model can't supply your real social proof or your actual story — that part is yours.
