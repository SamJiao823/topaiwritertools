The short answer: Rytr wins below about 2.8 pieces a month; Copy.ai wins above it. On writing they are level — both scored 7/10 on short-form in our blind test — so nothing about prose quality decides this. Rytr saves $16.50 a month on subscription and costs $5.83 more per piece in editing time, and that is the entire comparison. Solo writers producing a couple of pieces a month should take Rytr at $7.50. Anyone producing four or more should take Copy.ai despite it costing over three times as much.

The price gap, plainly

Rytr Unlimited is $7.50/mo billed annually, with a permanent free plan at 10K characters. Copy.ai's Chat plan is $29/mo month-to-month or $24/mo billed annually, covering up to five seats. That is a 3.2× difference on annual billing, and the sharpest value question in the budget category.

One change matters more than the prices: Copy.ai retired its free plan in 2026. If you are choosing between these two to test them without paying, Rytr is now the only one you can try. More detail is in our monthly cost breakdown.

Short-form: the tie that decides it

This is where the comparison resolves, and it resolves to nothing. On product descriptions, cold emails, and social captions, our blind scoring put both tools at 7/10. Two points below Claude, one below ChatGPT, and level with each other.

That matters because it removes quality from the decision entirely. There is no prose argument for paying 3.2× more. The only argument left is speed and workflow, and both of those are measurable — see below.

Long-form: where they actually differ

Copy.ai scored 6/10 on long-form against Rytr's 3/10. Three points is the largest gap between these two, and it is the one real quality difference in the comparison: Rytr's output degrades notably faster as pieces get longer, matching the pattern we documented in why AI long-form fails.

Be careful how much weight that carries, though. 6/10 is still two points below ChatGPT and three below Claude, both at $20 or less. If long-form is your main output, neither tool on this page is the right purchase — the honest answer is that the budget tier tops out before the long-form threshold does.

What Copy.ai's extra money buys

  • Templates and workflows: Copy.ai ships far more ready-made tools, which is worth real money to a team that does not want to write prompts from scratch.
  • Team features: shared spaces, collaboration, and approvals. The Chat plan covers five seats — against Rytr, which sells individual accounts.
  • Longer-form headroom: 6/10 against 3/10, the difference between a tool you can push past 1,000 words and one you cannot.
  • Faster finishing: about 15 minutes a piece against Rytr's 22, which is the number that decides the money question below.

What Rytr's price actually saves

Comparisons stop at the sticker. Here is the same pair with a writer's hour priced at $50 and the subscription included:

At four pieces a month the tool that costs 3.2× more is $6.82 cheaper to run. Rytr saves $16.50/month on subscription and adds $5.83 of editing time to every piece. Divide one by the other and the break-even is about 2.8 pieces a month.

That is a much lower threshold than the price gap suggests. Below 2.8 pieces a month, Rytr is the better financial choice and the free plan settles it. Above it — which is where most people who buy a writing tool actually sit — Copy.ai's "expensive" plan has been the cheaper one the whole time, and the premium you thought you were avoiding was being paid in your own hours.

Which one to pick

  • Under about 2.8 pieces a month, solo. Rytr, and start on the free plan to confirm the workflow suits you. Our guide to Rytr's free plan limits covers what the 10K character allowance gets you.
  • Four or more pieces a month. Copy.ai on annual billing. It is cheaper in total at that volume, and it is the only one of the two that handles longer pieces.
  • You need more than one seat. Copy.ai. The Chat plan covers five seats; Rytr's pricing is per account, so a team of three changes the arithmetic further in Copy.ai's favour.
  • Long-form is the main output. Neither. Both sit below ChatGPT at $20 and Claude at $20 — see our Claude Pro review.

When neither is the right answer

You are buying for one specific output. If everything you write is product descriptions for a store, neither of these is shaped for it — Hypotenuse at $19/mo scored the same 7/10 on short-form and is built for exactly that job. Paying for breadth you will not use is the most common wasted dollar in this category.

You want the writing to be good. Neither leads on prose. Claude scored 9/10 on both short and long-form at $20 and edited fastest in our test. If quality is the constraint rather than volume or workflow, this comparison is the wrong one to be reading.

You are not publishing yet. Both break-evens assume real volume. If you are buying because you might start writing more, wait until you actually have — and if you want to start now, Rytr's free plan costs nothing and Copy.ai no longer has one.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is better: Rytr or Copy.ai?

On writing, neither — both scored 7/10 on short-form in our blind test. Rytr is better below about 2.8 pieces a month on total cost and has a permanent free plan; Copy.ai is better above that volume, on long-form (6/10 against 3/10), and for teams needing multiple seats.

Is Copy.ai worth the extra money over Rytr?

Above roughly 2.8 pieces a month, yes — its faster editing makes it cheaper to run in total at that volume despite costing 3.2× more. Below that volume, no: Rytr wins on total cost, and the 7/10 short-form score is identical either way.

Can Rytr replace Copy.ai?

For short-form below about 2.8 pieces a month, yes — the output scored identically, and Rytr has the free plan. It cannot replace Copy.ai for team workflows, multiple seats, or anything past about 1,000 words, where it scored 3/10 on long-form against Copy.ai's 6/10.

Does Copy.ai have a free plan?

No. Copy.ai retired its free plan in 2026; its entry point is the Chat plan at $29/month monthly or $24/month billed annually, covering up to five seats. Rytr still has a permanent free plan at 10K characters a month.

Is Rytr good for long-form?

No. It scored 3/10 on long-form — the lowest mark in our benchmark. Copy.ai scored 6/10, which is better but still two points below ChatGPT and three below Claude, both of which cost $20 or less. For long articles, neither budget tool is the answer.

How many pieces a month before Copy.ai becomes cheaper than Rytr?

About 2.8. Rytr saves $16.50 a month on subscription but costs $5.83 more per piece in editing time at a $50/hour rate, so the saving is used up at just under three pieces a month. At four pieces a month Copy.ai costs $74.00 in total against Rytr's $80.82.

Which is faster to finish a piece with?

Copy.ai, by about seven minutes a piece — 15 minutes of editing against Rytr's 22. At a $50/hour rate that is $12.50 of your time against $18.33, a $5.83 gap on every piece. It is the single number that decides which of these two is cheaper.
