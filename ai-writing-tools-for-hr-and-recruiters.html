What HR actually writes

The writing load is bigger than most people think: job descriptions, candidate outreach emails, rejection notes, interview feedback, and employer-brand social posts. Our 30-day test focused on the three highest-volume tasks: job posts, outreach, and interview feedback.

Job descriptions: template-friendly, but watch the bias

Job posts are the easiest win. A structured prompt — 'Write a job description for [role]. Skills: [list]. Culture: [2-3 traits]. Emphasize [what differentiates].' — produces a clean draft in seconds. ChatGPT's free tier handled this well in testing.

The caveat that matters: research on gendered language in job posts is real. Ask the model to 'avoid gender-coded language' explicitly, and have a human review the final version. That's a compliance step, not a style preference.

Candidate outreach

Outreach emails are pure short-form, and our data says short-form is where budget tools hold up. Rytr at $7.50 produced outreach about 90% as good as Jasper at $39. The prompt pattern from testing: role + the candidate's specific signal (where you found them, what caught your eye) + one line about the role + one question. Never send the model's first draft without inserting the specific personalization — that's the whole game.

Interview feedback and notes

Interview feedback is sensitive and legally charged. Our recommendation from testing: use AI for the structure (formatting notes consistently), never for the judgment itself. Drafting a 'strengths / growth areas / verdict' template and filling it yourself is the safe workflow. Don't let a model interpret candidate responses — the stakes are too high.

Employer branding: where the upgrade pays off

Employer-brand content — culture posts, values pages, LinkedIn content — needs a consistent voice across many pieces. That's the exact scenario where our 30-day test showed Claude and Jasper pull ahead: they held a tone across a whole month of content, while budget tools drifted. If your company posts regularly about culture, the $20+ tier is defensible.

The tools, compared

From our 30-day test:

  • ChatGPT (free) — job posts, outreach, templates. Strong starting point.
  • Rytr ($7.50) — bulk short-form templates; strongest value per dollar for volume.
  • Claude Pro ($20) — employer-brand voice consistency and nuanced feedback drafting.
  • Jasper ($39) — shared brand voice for bigger teams; hard to justify for solo recruiters.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can AI write job descriptions that attract the right candidates?

Yes, with a structured prompt and a human pass. The key instruction is to avoid gender-coded language — that's a real research-backed bias — and to emphasize what actually differentiates the role. ChatGPT's free tier handles this well.

Is it safe to use AI for interview feedback?

Use AI for formatting and structure only, never for the judgment itself. Drafting consistent feedback templates and filling them yourself is the safe workflow. Don't let a model interpret candidate responses — the stakes are too high.

How do I choose an AI tool for recruiter outreach emails?

For short-form outreach, Rytr at $7.50 produced about 90% of Jasper's quality in our test. The personalization — the specific detail about why you reached out to this candidate — always has to be added by a human.

Are AI writing tools good for employer branding?

Yes, if you need a consistent voice across many pieces. Claude Pro and Jasper held a consistent tone across a month of culture content in our testing; budget tools drifted and needed re-specifying.
