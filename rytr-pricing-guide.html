Short answer: Rytr has three plans in 2026 — Free (10,000 characters/month), Unlimited ($9/mo monthly, or $7.50/mo billed annually at $90/year), and Premium ($29/mo). The annual Unlimited rate is the only one we would pay for writing: $90 pays for itself after 10 months of $9 billing. What 10,000 Free Characters a Month Actually Gets You

Rytr meters the free plan in characters, not words, which makes the ceiling hard to feel until you hit it. Converting is our arithmetic, not a Rytr-published figure: at roughly six characters per English word including the space, 10,000 characters is about 1,600–1,700 words. Published conversions that assume five characters per word land closer to 2,000, so treat 1,500–2,000 words as the working range and check the counter in Rytr's editor, which is the only authoritative number.

Read that table as a constraint, not a feature list. Free is not "a bit of everything each month" — it is one meaningful piece of work, and a 2,000-word article never fits at all. Where it genuinely works is narrow: short-form, in small batches, while you learn the editor. Our free plan breakdown goes deeper, and Rytr for short-form shows where the tool is strong.

When the Free Tier Stops Being Enough

  • You publish more than one piece a month. Two 900-word pieces already break the cap. Anyone with a content calendar outgrows free in month one.
  • You revise. Every discarded generation still came out of the meter. Whether regenerating spends characters again is something we could not verify as of September 2026 — but budget as if it does, because a piece rewritten three times can cost three times its length.
  • You use Rytr inside a workflow. Twenty product descriptions is one afternoon and 10,000 characters. Twenty a week is Unlimited.

The upgrade question is therefore not "is $9 a lot of money" — it is "do I write more than one thing a month". If yes, free is a trial, not a plan.

💵 The break-even is 10 months

$90 ÷ $9 = 10 months. Stay on Rytr for ten months or longer and the annual plan is strictly cheaper — you save $18 per year, about 16.7% off the monthly rate. Cancel before month ten and monthly billing would have cost you less, because you are not carrying the unused prepaid balance.

Framed per day, $90/year is about 25 cents a day for unlimited characters. Framed against output, if you publish four short-form pieces a week that is 208 pieces a year, or roughly 43 cents per piece in subscription cost before you count a single minute of editing.

That last clause is the honest caveat: cheap per word is not cheap per finished piece. We averaged ~22 minutes of editing per 1,500-word piece with Rytr against ~8 minutes with Claude or Jasper — at a $30/hour blended rate, about $11 in editing time, more than a month of the subscription. The arithmetic is in what AI writing tools really cost; the method is on How We Test.

Two terms we could not verify as of September 2026, and both matter before you prepay $90: whether the annual plan auto-renews at the same price, and whether it is refundable pro-rata if you cancel mid-term. Check them on Rytr's checkout before committing. Our refund and cancellation comparison tracks this across tools.

Premium at $29/mo: What Would Have to Be True

At $29/mo, Premium is 3.2x the monthly Unlimited price and 3.9x the annual effective rate — $348 a year against $90. For that to be sensible, Premium has to give you something you would otherwise buy separately, or cover work that Unlimited's 50 plagiarism checks cannot. Because we could not verify the feature delta (see the note above), the honest thing to publish is a decision rule rather than a verdict:

  • If the only reason you are considering Premium is "more writing", don't buy it. Rytr's tiers differ in quota and bundled tools rather than the generation engine. A bigger allowance buys more output, not better output — and the prose we scored was 7/10 short-form and 3/10 long-form regardless of tier.
  • If you need more bundled tools — more plagiarism checks, more brand voices, priority support — that is a different question, and it is exactly the question we could not answer from public sources. Verify the current Premium comparison on Rytr's pricing page before paying.
  • If you are comparing $29 elsewhere, the comparison is unkind to Premium: $29/mo is more than Claude Pro or ChatGPT Plus ($20/mo each), both of which out-scored Rytr on long-form in our benchmark and both of which have free tiers.

Our read, stated as a read: Premium is the hardest of the three plans to justify, and the most useful thing you can do before buying it is confirm on Rytr's own site what it adds. If that confirmation is hard to find, that is itself information.

The 50 Plagiarism Checks — and How Fast You'll Burn Them

Unlimited includes 50 plagiarism checks per month, worth sanity-checking against your publishing rate. Fifty checks across roughly 21 working days is about 2.4 checks per working day: publish two pieces a day, one check each, and you stay inside it. Check drafts as well as final versions and 50 disappears inside a week.

The free plan's plagiarism allowance is not something we could verify as of September 2026. The old version of this page listed 20 checks for the $7.50 tier and 100 for the $29 tier, but both numbers were tied to the retired plan names, so treat them as expired.

One distinction trips people up more than the quota: a plagiarism check is not an AI-detector check. A plagiarism checker asks whether text was copied from an existing source; it says nothing about whether an AI detector will flag a draft as machine-written. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does Rytr cost in 2026?

Rytr has three plans. Free is $0 with 10,000 characters per month. Unlimited is $9/mo monthly or $7.50/mo billed annually ($90/year). Premium is $29/mo. The annual Unlimited rate is the only one we would pay, and it pays for itself after 10 months.

Is Rytr's free plan enough to write a blog post?

For one blog post a month, yes. 10,000 characters is roughly 1,500-2,000 words, so a single 1,500-word post consumes nearly the whole allowance. For anything more than one piece a month, free runs out fast.

Is Rytr Unlimited worth $9/mo?

At the monthly price it is fair; at the annual rate of $7.50/mo it is the best value in Rytr's lineup — but only for short-form work, where it scored 7/10 in our 30-day benchmark. For long-form we do not recommend Rytr on any plan.

Is Rytr Premium worth $29/mo?

Not for better writing. Rytr's plans differ in quota and bundled tools rather than the generation engine, so Premium buys more output, not better output. We could not verify the full feature gap between Unlimited and Premium.

Does Rytr still have a Saver plan?

No. Saver is a retired plan name. The 2026 lineup is Free, Unlimited ($9/mo monthly or $7.50/mo annually) and Premium ($29/mo). Any guide that still lists Saver is out of date.

Can I cancel Rytr anytime?

Monthly billing can be cancelled from the account dashboard, and the free plan needs no card. We could not verify whether the prepaid $90 annual plan is refundable mid-term, so check the terms before prepaying.
