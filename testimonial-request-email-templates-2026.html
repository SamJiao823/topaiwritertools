Testimonial Requests That Come Back With Words

A testimonial request sent at the wrong moment, or asking for too much, is the reason most "please review us" emails get archived unread. These templates cover the three asks that return usable quotes — right after delivery, after a win, and from a long-term client — with the wording that makes saying yes easy.

The two things that kill testimonial requests are bad timing and an open-ended ask. "Say something nice about us" returns either silence or a sentence no one would publish. "Tick one of these and confirm?" returns a quote you can actually use.

The second lever is asking while the good feeling is current. A request sent three months after the job is a favour; sent three days after, it is a natural continuation of the interaction.

1. Post-delivery request

Use when: 2–4 days after you delivered, while the result is still in front of them.

Subject: Could you spare 30 seconds?

Hi [name], hope [deliverable] is doing what you needed. If it is, would you mind leaving a one-line note here: [review link]? It helps people who are deciding whether to work with us.

If something is not right, reply to this email instead — we would rather fix it than hear about it later.

Why this works: it offers an exit (tell us if it is wrong) which makes the public ask feel safe, and it names a tiny effort.

2. After a win

Use when: the customer has just told you the result was good — in an email, a call, anywhere.

Subject: Re: [their message] — can we quote you?

You said [quote their words]. Would you be comfortable with us using that, attributed to you, as a short testimonial? One reply is enough.

Why this works: you are asking permission to use their own words, which is far easier to grant than writing fresh praise.

3. Long-term client reference

Use when: a client has stayed with you across a renewal or a milestone.

Subject: Could we feature you as a case note?

[name], you have been with us since [date]. If you are open to it, we drafted a short note about the work — can you confirm it is accurate, or adjust anything? [draft link]

Why this works: doing the writing for them removes the only real cost (their time), so the confirmation rate is high.

What not to do

Do not offer to pay for testimonials — it breaks trust and in some places the rules. Do not edit their words into something they did not say; if you must trim, send the trimmed version for confirmation. Do not bulk-send to your whole list at once; target the people who actually had a good outcome.

Editing time, from our 30-day benchmark

Adapting a template to one customer: 2–4 minutes. Writing the long-term-client draft they confirm: 8–12 minutes, because the facts have to be exactly right or the confirmation stalls. That accuracy is the whole point of the approach.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the right time to ask for a testimonial?

Two to four days after delivery, while the result is still current, or the moment a customer tells you something worked. Asking months later turns a natural request into a favour.

How do I make it easy to say yes?

Minimise the effort: offer a one-click review link, ask permission to use their own words, or draft the note yourself and request confirmation. The cheaper the action, the higher the return.

Is it okay to pay for testimonials?

No. Paying for them undermines the trust they are meant to build, and disclosure rules in several regions treat it as problematic. Earn them with timing and ease instead.

What if a customer gives negative feedback instead?

Thank them and fix it through a reply, not a public request. A private channel for problems is exactly what a good request offers as an escape hatch.

Can AI help write the request?

Yes, for the structure and the phrasing. It cannot pick which customer actually had a good outcome, so the targeting stays your judgement.
