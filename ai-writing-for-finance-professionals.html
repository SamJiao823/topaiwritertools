The Short Answer

The short answer: ChatGPT free or Claude covers market summaries and first-draft reports well if you feed them the data — they don't invent numbers when prompted properly, but you must still verify every figure. For client emails and shorter updates, Rytr at $7.50/mo is the efficient pick. In our tests the tools differed far less than the discipline around them: data in, verify out is the whole workflow.

Where AI genuinely helps in finance writing

After 30 days, three tasks earned their keep: first-draft client updates (turning your bullet points into a clear email), plain-language explanations of complex products for clients, and report structure (lifting a pile of numbers into a narrative skeleton you then fill). Each tool handled these, but there's a hard rule we test everything against:

Numbers in the prompt must equal numbers in the output. We ran every finance test with figures explicitly provided — and we still verified each one. A tool that guesses a number for you is worse than useless in this industry.

The compliance-safe workflow we tested

Here's the process we settled on — it's reproducible and keeps you on the right side of review requirements:

  1. Draft from your notes, not from a blank ask. Provide the facts, figures, and points you want covered. Tools draft far better from a brief.
  2. Never ask the tool to 'add numbers'. Tell it explicitly: 'Do not invent figures; only use what I provide.'
  3. Flag anything uncertain. Prompt it to mark claims it can't support with the data given.
  4. Human review pass. A licensed professional signs off. The AI is a drafting assistant; the accountability is yours.
  5. Watch confidentiality. No client positions, PII, or non-public data in consumer tools.

The prompts that worked for us

  • Client update: 'Turn these bullet points into a 150-word client email. Lead with the one headline they care about. Use only the figures provided — do not add any.'
  • Market summary: 'Write a 200-word summary of this week's market note for a non-expert client. No jargon without a one-line explanation.'
  • Product explanation: 'Explain [product] to a new client in plain English. Cover how it works, the fee, and one risk. Under 180 words.'
  • Report skeleton: 'Turn these figures into an executive summary outline: key takeaway, what changed, why it matters, next steps.'

Claude gave the most natural non-expert explanations in our tests; ChatGPT free handled everything else fine.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it safe to use AI for financial writing?

It's safe when you control the inputs: provide the data, tell the tool not to invent figures, and verify every number before it goes out. Never put client positions or non-public information into consumer tools. The drafting is AI; the accountability is human.

Can AI write financial reports without making up numbers?

Yes, if you instruct it explicitly. Our tested prompt — 'only use the figures I provide, do not invent any' — held up across 30 days of tests. Even then, we verified every figure. Treat AI output as a draft with zero presumption of accuracy.

How do I choose an AI tool for client emails in finance?

For short client updates, a budget tool like Rytr ($7.50/mo) covers most needs efficiently. For tricky tone or explaining complex products, Claude ($20) produced the most natural drafts in our tests. Start free, pay when you hit a wall.
