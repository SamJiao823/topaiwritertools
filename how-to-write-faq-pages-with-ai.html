An FAQ page looks like the easiest content on the internet to write — and the easiest to do wrong. Done well, it answers real customer questions with direct, self-contained answers and earns visibility in search results. Done badly, it's a wall of guessed questions nobody asked. This guide covers the structure that makes the difference, how to source the questions, and where AI actually helps.

1. FAQ Pages Are Structured Content

An FAQ page is less an article than a data structure: questions and answers a search engine can parse. When you mark the page up with FAQPage schema, eligible queries can show your answers directly in the results — that's the payoff. But it only works if every answer is direct and self-contained, because a rich result shows a truncated version of your answer, not the whole page.

That structure is also what makes AI useful. Give the AI a defined shape — one question, one 40–60 word answer — and it fills the shape well. Give it an open-ended "write an FAQ" and it produces vague content that earns nothing.

2. Where Real Questions Come From

The most reliable source of FAQ questions is your own business, not your imagination. Pull the twenty questions you hear most often, group them, and pick the six to ten that actually matter for a purchase or signup decision.

Once you have the list, you own the questions. The AI's job is to improve the wording of the answers, not to invent the topics.

3. The Question-Writing Rule: Never Let AI Invent Questions

This is the rule that separates FAQ pages that rank from FAQ pages that get ignored. AI is great at rephrasing; it is bad at knowing what your customers actually ask. If you prompt it to "generate 10 common questions," you'll get generic ones that match no search intent and earn no rich results. Write the questions yourself from real sources, then hand only the wording to the AI.

This is the same principle we use for landing pages in our guide to writing landing pages with AI: start from real customer language, not invented phrasing. The questions are the customer's words; the polish is the AI's job.

4. Answers: 40–60 Words, Direct First

Each answer should open with the direct answer in the first sentence, then add one or two supporting sentences. Google's guidance favors concise answers that work standalone, and the FAQPage rich result shows only a slice of your text. On our own pages, answers that run 40–60 words performed consistently — long enough to be useful, short enough to be shown.

Ask the AI to rewrite your draft answer to that length rather than writing from scratch. Give it your factual answer and one instruction: "Rewrite this in 40–60 words, starting with the direct answer." Then check that it added no facts of its own.

5. The FAQPage Schema Setup

The schema itself is small. Wrap each question-answer pair in a Question entity inside a mainEntity array, and mark the whole thing with the type FAQPage. Each Question has a name and an acceptedAnswer with the text. It is valid JSON-LD — the same format this page itself uses in its head, and the same structure we copy into every how-to template on the site.

Test the result after publishing. A structured FAQ that parses correctly can earn the rich result; a page without the markup is just a regular page with headings.

6. Use AI to Polish Wording, Not to Add Facts

The correct division of labor: you supply the questions and the facts; AI improves the wording and tightens the answer to length. If the AI adds a claim or a statistic, cut it — an FAQ answer with a fact you can't source is worse than no answer. 7. The FAQ Checklist

  • Questions pulled from real customer language, not AI-generated.
  • Direct answer first in every response.
  • Answers 40–60 words, readable standalone.
  • FAQPage schema present and valid JSON-LD.
  • No facts added by AI that you can't source.
  • Only questions people actually search — quality over quantity.

Run that checklist and the FAQ page becomes one of the higher-value pages on your site: it answers the questions that actually stop buyers, and it can earn visibility in the results themselves. That's the difference between writing FAQs for a checkbox and writing them for Google to use.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do FAQ pages help with SEO?

Yes, in two ways. First, the content targets long-tail questions you might not cover elsewhere. Second, FAQPage schema can make your answers eligible for rich results, which raises visibility on mobile search. The benefit depends on using real questions; generic FAQs from AI rarely match what people type.

Can Google penalize FAQ content written by AI?

No, and Google has said so directly. It evaluates whether the page helps people, not whether a machine wrote it. FAQ pages fail when the questions are invented, the answers are thin, or the content duplicates other pages. Real questions with direct 40-60 word answers avoid all three failure modes.

What is FAQPage schema and do I need it?

FAQPage schema is JSON-LD that tells search engines your Q&A is a structured FAQ. It can make eligible questions appear as rich results with your answer shown directly. You do not strictly need it, but without it a FAQ is just a regular page. We include a ready-to-copy version in each of our how-to templates.
