Which AI Writing Tools Offer Real Free Trials?

Most "free trial" claims fall into three buckets: a time-limited trial (7-14 days), a forever-free plan with word caps, or a refund-backed trial like Writesonic's 7-day window. After 30 days of real use, four tools stood out as worth signing up for: Writesonic, Jasper, Rytr, and Grammarly.

These four cover the whole spectrum — from drafting full articles to polishing existing copy — and they're the ones where the free access actually shows you the paid product, not a crippled demo. The rest of the market tends to hide the good models behind a paywall the moment you hit 2,000 words.

How We Tested These Free Trials Over 30 Days

We signed up for each tool on day one and used nothing but the free access for the full month. For every tool we tracked four things:

  • Free quota: how many words or characters you actually get, and whether it resets monthly.
  • Trial length: whether it's a time-limited trial or a forever-free plan.
  • Credit card: whether you can start without entering a card.
  • Real output: whether the free tier produces usable work or just lures you into an upsell.

All figures below were re-checked in August 2026. Trial terms do change, so treat the table as a starting point, not a contract.

What Do Free Trials Actually Deliver?

Writesonic — a 7-day trial, now GEO-focused

Writesonic no longer offers a permanent free plan. It now runs a 7-day trial with a 7-day refund, and it has repositioned as an AI Search Visibility (GEO) platform — so judge it on that, not on budget article drafting. For plain writing, Rytr at $7.50 or ChatGPT Plus at $20 is the honest pick.

Where it struggles: it's no longer aimed at general writing, so for drafting and paraphrasing the price-to-value math is worse than ChatGPT Plus or Rytr. Consider it only when AI search visibility is the explicit goal.

Jasper — 7 days of full access, if you hand over a card

Jasper's trial is the most premium-feeling of the four: full features, brand voice, and a polished editor for one week. The catch is the credit card — it's the only one here that requires one up front. If you cancel before day seven, you're not charged.

For a deep, hands-on test of a "main" tool you plan to pay for, that week is genuinely useful. For casual evaluation, Rytr is less friction — Writesonic only makes sense if AI search visibility is the goal.

Rytr — a free tier that's actually usable long-term

Rytr's forever-free plan gives you 10,000 characters a month — roughly 1,500 to 2,000 words. That's small, but it resets monthly and never expires, and it's the same quality as the paid product. We wrote real drafts on it for the whole 30 days without hitting a wall mid-month.

The honesty here: 2,000 words a month is not enough for professional volume. It's a genuinely usable free plan, not a full replacement.

Grammarly — the free version is enough for most editing

Grammarly's free version has no word cap and no credit card. What it lacks is the advanced rewrites, tone, and plagiarism checks of Premium. For our test, the free tier caught the same typos and clarity issues that matter for everyday email and copywriting.

If your job is fixing text someone else drafted, Grammarly free is probably all you need. If you want it to rewrite your sentences for you, that's the Premium upsell.

Which Free Trial Should You Try First?

Don't sign up for all four at once — you'll spread your testing thin and never form an honest opinion. Here's the path we'd run again:

  1. Start with Rytr for volume. Its free plan is small but honest, and $7.50/mo later is the cheapest paid option in the list.
  2. Use Writesonic's 7-day trial only if AI search visibility (GEO) is the goal — it's a $79/mo platform, not a budget writing tool.
  3. Use Jasper's 7-day trial only when you're ready to decide. Save the card requirement for when a purchase is genuinely on the table.
  4. Run Grammarly free in the background for editing. It costs nothing and improves everything you write, regardless of which generator you pick.

This order matters: it front-loads the free, no-card tools so you build an opinion before any trial clock or paywall pressure starts.

Is a Free Trial Enough, or Should You Go Paid?

Honest answer: for most people, the free tier is the right place to start, but the paid plan is where the tool becomes a habit. A 2,000-word cap is fine for testing; it's painful for publishing week after week.

Our recommendation after 30 days:

  • If your goal is AI search visibility (GEO), Writesonic's 7-day trial tells you more than any comparison table — but at $79/mo it only pays off for that goal, not for plain drafting.
  • If you write lightly or just want grammar help, Rytr's free plan and Grammarly's free version may genuinely be enough.
  • If you need premium output for client work, Jasper's week-long trial is the fairest way to judge it before committing.

Try the free path first. The tools that keep you coming back are the ones worth paying for.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which AI writing tool has the strongest free trial?

Writesonic and Jasper both run 7-day trials — Writesonic adds a 7-day refund window, while Jasper requires a credit card up front. For long-term free use, Rytr's free plan is the most practical.

Do AI writing tool free trials require a credit card?

Not always. Rytr and Grammarly all let you start without a credit card. Jasper's 7-day trial does require one — and Writesonic no longer has a free plan, only a 7-day trial.

Which AI writing tools are free without a credit card?

Rytr's forever-free plan and Grammarly's free version work without a credit card. Writesonic no longer offers a permanent free plan — just a 7-day trial with a 7-day refund.

How many words can you write with a free AI writing tool?

Rytr gives 10,000 characters (~1,500-2,000 words) per month on its free plan, and Grammarly's free version has no word cap but limits suggestions. Writesonic no longer offers a free word quota — just a 7-day trial.

Can I combine free trials to avoid paying?

You can, but we don't recommend relying on it. Free tiers have real limits, and switching tools every month costs more in time than the monthly fee does. Test 2-3 tools, then commit to one.
