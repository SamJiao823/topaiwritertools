Cold Email Templates: 6 Structures, and the Lines You Have to Rewrite Yourself

A cold email template is useful for the shape and useless for the sentence. We tested six structures over thirty days of outreach and kept the ones that produced replies, along with a note on which line in each one has to be written by a human.

Every template below works for the same reason and fails for the same reason. It works because structure is the hard part of a cold email — where the reason goes, how short the ask is, when to stop. It fails when the sentence about them is generic, because that sentence is the whole email. Fill the bracketed lines yourself; those are the ones that decide whether it gets read.

Before you use any of them

  • One reason per email. A cold email with three reasons reads like a brochure. Pick the strongest and cut the rest.
  • The ask must be small. "Fifteen minutes" is a small ask. "A partnership" is not. Replies go to small asks.
  • Under 120 words. Every template below fits. If yours does not, cut the paragraph about you.
  • A real unsubscribe or opt-out line for bulk sends. Required in most markets and skipped by most templates.

1. The specific observation

Best when you can see something they published, shipped or changed recently.

Subject: [the thing you noticed, in four words]

Hi [first name],

[SENTENCE YOU MUST WRITE — the specific thing you saw, and why it matters to them.]

We do [one line, what you actually do] for [the kind of company they are]. [One proof point: a number, a name, or a short outcome.]

Worth fifteen minutes to see whether it applies to you?

[Your name]

Rewrite this line: the observation. "Saw your post about X" is not an observation. "Your pricing page lists four tiers and none mention annual" is.

2. The peer reference

Best when you have a comparable customer and permission to name the category, if not the name.

Subject: [their category] + [the outcome]

Hi [first name],

We work with [number] [their category] companies on [the narrow problem]. [NAMED COMPANY OR CATEGORY] cut [metric] from [x] to [y] in [timeframe].

Not sure it applies to you — [ONE SENTENCE ASKING A REAL QUESTION ABOUT THEIR SETUP].

If it does, I will send the two-page version. If not, I will stop here.

[Your name]

Rewrite this line: the question about their setup. A genuine question gets a reply from people who would ignore a pitch.

3. The follow-up that admits it is a follow-up

For the second and third touch. Most follow-ups pretend they are new emails, which everyone can tell.

Subject: Re: [original subject]

Hi [first name] — following up once on the below.

[ONE NEW SENTENCE: something that changed, a number, or a short piece of evidence.] Nothing else.

If the timing is wrong, say the word and I will come back in [month].

[Your name]

Rewrite this line: the new sentence. A follow-up with no new information is a nudge, and nudges get archived.

4. The close-out

The fourth email, the one that usually gets the most replies relative to effort.

Subject: closing this out

Hi [first name],

I have reached out a few times and heard nothing, which usually means [not a priority / wrong person / wrong time].

I will stop here. If it becomes relevant, [WHERE TO FIND YOU] — or reply with a name and I will take it to the right person.

[Your name]

Rewrite this line: the three options. Naming them makes replying feel like choosing rather than refusing.

5. The referral path

When you have a mutual connection or a shared context.

Subject: [mutual connection] suggested I write

Hi [first name],

[MUTUAL CONNECTION] mentioned you were dealing with [specific problem]. We have done [narrow thing] for [company type] — [one outcome, one number].

If [CONNECTION] got it wrong, ignore me. If not, fifteen minutes?

[Your name]

Rewrite this line: the connection's name. Do not use this structure without a real one — it is the easiest email to verify and the fastest to be caught on.

6. The resource, with nothing attached

When you have something genuinely useful and are willing to give it without a meeting.

Subject: [the resource, stated plainly]

Hi [first name],

[ONE SENTENCE: what it is and who it is for.] No meeting needed — [LINK].

If it is useful, [WHAT THEY SHOULD DO NEXT]. If not, no reply needed.

[Your name]

Rewrite this line: the link. Attachments in cold email reduce delivery; a plain link to something real does not.

The lines that get a cold email flagged

  • Praise that could apply to anyone. "Love what you are building" is the most common opener in a flagged email.
  • Urgency with no deadline. "Limited time" with no date reads as pressure tactics to both filters and people.
  • Attachments on a first touch. Keep the first email text-only.
  • No physical address or opt-out on bulk sends. A legal requirement in several markets, and a spam signal everywhere.
  • Volume spikes from a new domain. Ramp a new sending domain slowly; the templates will not save you from a reputation problem.

Why generated openers fail

A model has never met the recipient, so the sentence it produces about them is a description of the category, not of the person. That is usable as a starting shape and unusable as the final line. In our thirty days, the emails that got replies shared one property: the second sentence could not have been sent to anyone else. That sentence is the whole job, and it is the one part worth writing slowly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do cold email templates still work in 2026?

The structure does, the sentences do not. A template solves where the reason goes and how short the ask should be; the line about the recipient still has to be written by you.

How long should a cold email be?

Under 120 words. Every template here fits, and if yours does not, the paragraph to cut is the one about you.

Should I attach anything to a first email?

No. Text-only on the first touch, with a plain link if you have something to share. Attachments reduce deliverability.

How many follow-ups are reasonable?

Two working follow-ups and one close-out. After that, the honest move is to stop and come back later with a real reason.
