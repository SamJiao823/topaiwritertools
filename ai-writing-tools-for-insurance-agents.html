Insurance copy carries weight. A sentence that implies a cover exists when it does not is a complaint, and a complaint is a regulator's problem. That makes the split between safe and unsafe AI work unusually clear.

Safe to hand over

  • Lead magnets and comparison pages. "What a typical home policy excludes" written as education, not as a quote.
  • Follow-up sequences. Three emails after an abandoned quote form, each answering one real objection.
  • Plain-language explainers. "What excess means" done in 200 words. Your compliance team checks the facts; the model does the explaining.
  • Internal briefs and call notes. Summarising what a prospect said so the next meeting starts informed.
  • Client FAQ pages. Pulled from the questions your inbox already answers every week.

Where it must stop

  • Anything that quotes a price or a figure. Premiums, excesses, limits, terms. These change daily and are yours, not a language model's.
  • Coverage promises. "This policy covers flood damage" when the policy schedule says otherwise. Never generated as a sentence.
  • Regulatory wording.-disclosure sentences, cooling-off language, complaint routes. Use the exact approved text.
  • Claims handling statements. How a claim is assessed depends on the insurer's process, not on a model's guess.

Where the time actually goes

In our 30-day test the budget dedicated writer needed about 22 minutes of editing per 1,000 words against roughly 8 minutes for the premium tool — about $7 per piece at $30/hour. A producer who writes explainers and follow-ups daily gets that time back; one who writes nothing but polish does not.

A workflow that survives review

Build a facts library first: your carrier's actual coverage wording, excess structure, exclusions, and the approved disclosure block. Every prompt cites it. Then treat every generated sentence about cover as needing a sign-off — yours or compliance's — before it goes on a public page.

The result is a producer who spends the saved time on the phone, which is the only place insurance actually gets sold.

What agents ask

Can AI write insurance policy explanations?

Yes, in plain language — from your own policy wording. Never let it infer coverage from a general description of the product category.

Is content written by AI a compliance risk?

The content, not the tooling. Any statement about cover, price or claim process needs review before it publishes.

Which tool for an agency?

Frequently Asked Questions

Can AI explain an insurance policy?

Yes in plain language, if you feed it the actual policy wording. Never from general knowledge of the category.

Is AI-written insurance content a compliance risk?

The statements are. Cover, price and claims wording need sign-off before publishing.

How do I keep quotes accurate?

Keep a facts library with real figures and citation rules. A model will otherwise invent a plausible premium.
