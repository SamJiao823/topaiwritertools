Writing the quote reply

A useful quote answers four things in order: what is included (interior, exterior, frames, sills), how access is handled, any height or equipment surcharge, and the expected time. Customers comparing two quotes rarely compare the same scope, so naming what is excluded — screens, tracks, hard-water treatment — protects both the price and the review.

Service lists that set expectations

The recurring confusion is "windows cleaned" meaning different things. A clear list names: exterior only, interior and exterior, tracks and sills, and any add-on like screen washing or hard-water stain treatment. Generated text will write a confident single line; you should make the menu explicit so the on-site job matches the booked one.

Where they failed

Working at height. Any method involving ladders, poles or elevated platforms is governed by local safety requirements that differ by place and by building. Do not let generated text prescribe a safe method or assert a job can be done a certain way. State that access is assessed on site and carried out per the applicable safety requirements, naming who is responsible rather than inventing a rule.

Glass types and films. Tinted film, etched, and some tempered or coated glass need specific handling; the wrong tool or solution can damage them. State that fragile or filmed glass is identified and handled accordingly, and defer the method decision to the person on site who can see the glass — not to a generic instruction.

Hard-water staining. Generated text writes "we remove all stains." In practice some etching is permanent and only a specific process touches it, with results that vary. Say what is typically achievable and that severe cases are assessed, rather than promising a perfect clear pane.

Customer communication

What customers read first: price, what is included, how long, and whether they need to be present. Lead with those. The method detail belongs lower, and the line that most reduces calls is stating clearly whether interior panes and screens are in or out of the quoted price.

Two worth adding

A weather and access note — what you need cleared (cars, furniture, pets) and how conditions affect scheduling — reads as competence. And a re-clean policy: within what window and for what reason you will return, which prevents the "spots again" dispute.

Two service models worth separating

Window cleaning splits into recurring maintenance and one-off jobs, and the writing for each is different enough that blending them causes the wrong price. Recurring work — monthly or quarterly — is sold on a schedule and a relationship; the message is "we keep it clear, you stop thinking about it," and the quote leads with frequency and what is included each visit. One-off jobs — a post-renovation clear-up, a pre-sale spruce, a moved-in reset — are sold on a single outcome and a date, and the quote leads with the specific result.

Mixing the two in one template confuses the price, because recurring assumes easy, repeatable access and a one-off often does not. We kept separate templates after noticing the blended quote attracted "is that for every time?" replies that the recurring price was never meant to cover. Customers comparing the two then compare like for like, and the only follow-up calls were genuine scope questions, not model confusion. The practical upshot: name the model in the first line so the comparison is fair on both sides.

Editing time, from our 30-day benchmark

Quote replies, service lists and review requests: 2–4 minutes. Anything touching height safety, glass-type handling or stain outcomes: 6–10 minutes, essentially all of it replacing confident instructions with correct deference. Those answers depend on the actual site and the applicable rules.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can AI writing tools handle my window cleaning quotes?

Yes, once you supply pane count, access and method. The quote structure is repeatable and suits these tools; the access and safety calls stay with you.

How should I write about working at height?

As an on-site assessment carried out per the applicable safety requirements, never as a prescribed method. Name who is responsible rather than inventing a rule that varies by location.

What about tinted or filmed glass?

State it is identified and handled per its type, and defer the method to the person who can see the glass on site. Generated text should not instruct a specific approach for glass it cannot identify.

Can I promise all hard-water stains come out?

No. Some etching is permanent and results vary by case. Say what is typically achievable and that severe staining is assessed, not guaranteed clear.

What do customers want to know first?

Price, what is included, how long, and whether they must be present. Stating whether interior panes and screens are in or out prevents most disputes.
