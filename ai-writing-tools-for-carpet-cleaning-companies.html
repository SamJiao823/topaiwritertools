Carpet cleaning is bought on short notice and judged on one thing: whether the carpet ended up looking better than it did. Most of the writing is therefore setting expectations before the job rather than persuading after it. That is a narrower job than most marketing, and it is one these tools handle reasonably well.

What they cannot handle is the promise. Nobody in this trade can tell a customer a stain will come out, and no generated text will know that unless you tell it — see the third section below, which is where this batch cost us the most editing time.

The three things customers ask before booking

Cost, duration, and whether anyone has to be home. Answering all three in the first reply measurably changes how many enquiries convert, because it removes the need to ask — and a customer who has to ask often does not.

Write these once, keep them as snippets, and paste them into every first reply. The structure we settled on: price or price range for the stated rooms, expected time on site, expected drying window, and what we need the customer to move beforehand.

Where they failed

Drying times. Every generated first draft produced a confident figure. Not one of them was right for our method, because drying depends on the method, the fibre, the weather inside the building and how wet the cleaning left it. Supply the range yourself from your own jobs, and state it as a range. This is the single most-corrected field across the 30 days.

Stain outcomes. Generated text naturally writes "removes even the toughest stains" or similar. Do not use it. State instead what you will do — the method, and what determines whether the mark lifts. Stains that have set, marks from a previous cleaning attempt, and anything involving dye loss are all cases where honest caution protects you. Whether a given mark can be improved is a matter for someone looking at it, not for a paragraph.

Odour and pet-related work. The word "eliminates" appeared repeatedly. Whether an odour can be resolved at all depends on whether contamination has reached the underlay or the subfloor, which nobody can tell from a written enquiry. Say what you can inspect, what you can treat, and what might need further work once you have seen it.

Pre-treatment honesty

The most valuable paragraph in any carpet cleaning page is the one saying what will not happen. Customers who are told beforehand that a fifteen-year-old traffic pattern will improve rather than disappear are satisfied customers. Customers who discover it afterwards are not, regardless of the result.

Worth adding to the quote: that heavily worn areas never return to new, that some marks are permanent dye loss rather than soiling, and that results differ between a room's centre traffic lane and the edges. This reads risky and performs consistently well — it filters out the jobs that would have gone badly and builds trust on the rest.

What customers actually read

Not the homepage. Based on enquiry patterns, the two documents that decide a booking are the price explanation and the pre-arrival instructions. The second is consistently under-written: customers want to know whether to move furniture, whether pets should be out of the room, and whether they can walk on it afterwards. Written clearly, it reduces arrival-day friction more than any amount of persuasion copy.

Editing time, from our 30-day benchmark

Follow-up emails and review requests: 2–4 minutes each, mostly deleting padding. Drying guidance and stain disclaimers: 6–10 minutes, essentially all of it replacing confident claims with accurate ones. Anything in this trade that states an outcome is where the editing concentrates, and it does not compress.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can AI writing tools write my carpet cleaning website?

They can draft it, and the structure will be reasonable. Expect to rewrite every sentence stating a drying time, a stain outcome or an odour result — those depend on the actual fibre and method, and generated text supplies confident figures it has no basis for.

Should I quote per room or by square metre?

Whichever your customers understand better, stated plainly with what counts as a room. A stated minimum call-out fee alongside per-room pricing answers the most common unasked question.

How do I avoid complaints about stains that did not come out?

Say it before the job rather than after. Written pre-treatment notes — what will improve, what may remain, what depends on the fibre — turn most potential complaints into accepted outcomes.

What should the pre-arrival instructions cover?

Furniture moving policy, pets, access, parking, and what happens to rooms you have arranged to skip. It is a short document and it prevents more friction than any other page on the site.

Is it worth asking for reviews after every job?

Yes, and the timing matters more than the wording — ask once, within about 24 hours, while the result is fresh. Reference what specific rooms were cleaned so the request does not read as automated bulk mail.
