Why Would You Need a Grammarly Alternative?

We spent 30 days using Grammarly on our own drafts and running it head-to-head against free and paid alternatives across three jobs: proofreading, rewriting, and writing from scratch. The pattern was consistent. Grammarly is excellent at one job — catching mistakes in text you already wrote — and does nothing at another — writing the text in the first place.

What Grammarly does well

  • Grammar, spelling, and punctuation. The core checking is reliable and fast across the browser, desktop apps, and Google Docs.
  • Tone and clarity nudges. The free tier suggests simpler phrasing and flags tone shifts, which genuinely improved our drafts.
  • Ubiquity. It is installed everywhere by default, so it catches errors in email, CMSes, and chat without extra steps.

Where it falls short

  • No content generation. Grammarly is a checker, not a writer. When you need a first draft, it is not the tool.
  • Premium is optional for many users. Tone, plagiarism, and advanced style live behind $12/mo (annual) — while free alternatives already cover part of that at zero cost.
  • Language coverage. LanguageTool supports far more languages on a free plan than Grammarly does.

The point is not that Grammarly is bad — it is that Grammarly answers one question ("is this clean?"), while most people searching for alternatives also have a second question ("can you write this?"). The tools below are grouped by which of those questions they answer.

How Do I Choose a Free Grammarly Alternative?

If your only problem is mistakes in text you already wrote, you should not have to pay to fix them.

LanguageTool — a strong free proofreader

LanguageTool's free plan checks grammar, spelling, and style, and in our 30 days it was noticeably stricter on punctuation and repeated words than Grammarly's free tier. It supports 30+ languages, and the free plan allows around 20,000 characters per check — enough for a full article in a few passes. Premium runs $4.99/mo billed annually for plagiarism detection and unlimited style checks. For pure proofreading without a budget, this is our pick.

Free tiers worth knowing

Grammarly's own free tier still handles basic grammar and spelling well — keep it if you rely on the tone suggestions. Writesonic's free plan gives 10,000 words a month and includes rewriting. LanguageTool free covers the most languages and the strictest style checking at zero cost.

A note on free plans: everything free is limited by design. If a tool is doing real work for you, the paid tier is usually where it becomes dependable.

Which Grammarly Alternative Is Suited for Writing?

This is the scenario Grammarly cannot help with at all. If you need text written for you, these two are the value options.

Rytr — the budget pick

At $7.50/mo on the Saver plan, Rytr is the most budget-friendly tool on this list that actually writes. Captions, emails, product bullets, and short posts come out clean enough to skip most proofreading. It is not a long-form tool — past roughly 1,000 words the structure starts to drift — so for anything longer, pair it with LanguageTool or Grammarly free.

Writesonic — the value pick

At $20/mo, Writesonic covers rewriting, full articles, and built-in SEO features, and its free tier (10,000 words/month) lets you test before paying. In our 30 days it was the closest drop-in replacement for a Grammarly-plus-writer stack: it cleans up your text and produces new text in the same subscription.

Bottom line for writing: Rytr if your output is short copy and budget rules; Writesonic if you need articles, rewriting, and proofreading in one plan.

Which Grammarly Alternative Combines Grammar and Writing?

Some teams do not want two subscriptions — one for clean prose and one for drafting. Only one tool here genuinely covers both.

Jasper — the premium pick

Jasper is the only tool in our batch that pairs a real editing layer with genuinely premium long-form writing. Brand Voice learns how you write across sessions, and SEO Mode pulls in search data before you start drafting. Creator starts at $39/mo billed annually, with a 7-day free trial. It is the most expensive option in this roundup — and the closest thing to answering both questions, "is this clean?" and "can you write this?" in one tool.

Caveat from our testing: Jasper is not a dedicated proofreader. For a final-line grammar pass, running LanguageTool free or Grammarly on top is still worth the thirty seconds.

How to Choose the Right Grammarly Alternative

One quick framework before you sign up for anything. Find the situation that sounds like yours:

  • You only need free proofreading → LanguageTool free, or keep Grammarly free if you rely on the tone suggestions.
  • You need short copy written for you → Rytr at $7.50/mo.
  • You need articles, rewriting, and proofreading → Writesonic at $20/mo.
  • You need premium long-form and brand voice → Jasper, on the 7-day trial.
  • You are happy with Grammarly → stay. An alternative only pays off when it closes a real gap.

Every tool on this list has a free tier or trial. Our 30-day advice: run the same real draft through the two candidates for your scenario and keep the one you would publish without rework.

Which Grammarly alternative is a strong fit for writing?

Not fully — Jasper is not a dedicated proofreader. But if you want strong writing and brand voice with an editing layer, Jasper gets closest to covering both grammar and writing in one tool.
