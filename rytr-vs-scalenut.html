Where Rytr Wins (and Where It Doesn't)

Rytr is the budget short-form king. In our 30-day test, it hit roughly 90% of Jasper's quality on emails, product descriptions, and captions — at about one-fifth the price. For high-volume short copy, the mental math stops being "can I afford it" and becomes "why would I hand-write this."

Where it falls apart is length. Past ~1,000 words, coherence slides (3/10), and editing averaged ~22 minutes per piece. That editing gap is the real cost: a "cheap" tool that costs an extra 15 minutes per article is usually a net loss if you publish weekly.

The Honest Gap: We Didn't Test Scalenut

Scalenut positions itself around SEO content — long-form, keyword planning, and SEO workflows. We haven't run a 30-day test on it, so we won't score it. If that's the use case pulling you toward Scalenut, the closest thing we have tested is Writesonic: the only budget tool with native SEO scoring + GEO built in (8/10 short, 7/10 long).

Who Should Choose What

  • Short-form on a budget → Rytr ($7.50). Nothing we tested beats it on value per dollar for emails and product copy.
  • SEO long-form on a budget → Writesonic ($20). Native SEO+GEO is the differentiator.
  • Long-form prose quality first → Claude (9/10 long-form in our test).
  • Brand voice at scale → Jasper ($49).
