Two document types carried the value: quotes that make preparation explicit, and cure instructions. A third — warranty terms — we removed from generation, and a fourth, substrate assessment, must be inspected rather than drafted.

Epoxy fails because of what is underneath, not what goes on top. A document that describes the coating without describing the substrate preparation sets up the wrong expectation, and when the floor fails eighteen months later, the quote is the first thing that gets re-read.

Where it helped

Quotes that itemise preparation

The structure worth reusing: surface condition, preparation method, number of coats, product system, cure time before foot traffic, cure time before vehicles, and exclusions. The preparation line is the one that protects you — grinding versus acid etching, crack repair, moisture mitigation — and it is the line a generic draft will blur into "surface preparation".

Drafting fell from about 25 minutes to 7. The itemisation carried over between jobs with only the specifics changing.

Cure and return-to-service instructions

When can they walk on it, when can they move the fridge back, when can they park, and what voids the finish. Generated drafts handled this well because the sequence is standard and the phrasing is not. The one required edit: the times must come from your product system and the conditions on the day, not from the draft's defaults. Temperature and humidity change cure windows substantially.

Preparation expectations sent before arrival

Clear the space, disconnect and move appliances, confirm power and water availability, arrange for pets to be elsewhere, and note any known moisture issues. Generated checklists were consistently more complete than what we had been sending, and each missing item costs time on site.

Where it failed

Substrate and moisture assessment

Drafts described concrete condition and moisture behaviour as though assessed. Whether a slab is suitable — moisture vapour emission rate, previous coatings, contamination — is measured on site with the right equipment. In our testing the drafts confidently stated that a floor was ready for coating when nobody had tested it. This category is inspection-only.

Cure times and chemical specifics

Generated text gave precise cure windows, mixing ratios and recoat intervals. These come from the product data sheet for the specific system being used, and they shift with conditions. Keep them in a maintained document and have the instructions cite it.

Warranty and performance claims

Drafts stated coverage periods and performance expectations — resistance to chemicals, abrasion, hot tyre pick-up. These are commitments the business has to honour, and what can be promised depends on the system, the substrate and the use. All warranty wording stays in the maintained document, owned by a named person.

Health and ventilation guidance

Generated instructions covered ventilation, exposure and protective equipment. That is safety information and it comes from the safety data sheet and your own procedures — not from a draft summarising general practice.

What customers actually check

Someone getting a garage or workshop floor coated wants to know three things: how long before they can use it, how it will be prepared, and what the warranty covers. In our testing, quotes that addressed all three explicitly drew fewer price objections than cheaper quotes that left them out.

One addition worth making: a short note on what the surface will look like day to day — gloss level, how it shows dust, how it handles spills. Unmet expectations about appearance generate more complaints than performance problems.

What the quote should say about the substrate

The most important line in an epoxy quote is not the coating — it is what is underneath and what preparation that requires. State the substrate condition as observed, the preparation method it calls for, and what happens if conditions differ once work starts. In our testing, quotes that made preparation a separate itemised line drew far fewer disputes than quotes that folded it into a single price, even when the total was the same.

Editing time, from our 30-day benchmark

Quotes went from roughly 25 minutes to 7, near $9 saved at $30 per hour. Cure instructions dropped from about 14 minutes to 4. Preparation checklists from 10 to 2. Warranty content was not generated.

A workflow that holds up

Template the quote with preparation as its own itemised line. Generate cure instructions from a fixed sequence, then confirm every timing against the product data sheet and the day's conditions. Send the preparation checklist at booking. Substrate and moisture assessment, cure times, warranty terms and safety guidance come from your own documentation and inspection — measured on site, maintained by a named person, and written by whoever is accountable.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can AI write our epoxy quotes?

The structure, yes — provided preparation is itemised from the site assessment rather than summarised generically.

Should it describe substrate condition?

No. Moisture and contamination are measured on site. This is inspection-only content.

Can it state cure times?

Only from your product data sheet, adjusted for conditions on the day. Never use a draft's defaults.

What about warranty terms?

Keep them in a maintained document owned by a named person, and link to it.

Should it cover ventilation and safety?

Only from the safety data sheet and your own procedures.

Is this useful for a small crew?

The preparation checklist and cure instructions pay back first — both prevent callbacks.
