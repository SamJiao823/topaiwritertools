How to Write a Newsletter with AI

A newsletter that keeps subscribers needs a voice, not just words. We spent 30 days testing a weekly workflow where AI does the heavy lifting and your voice does the differentiating. Here's the exact process.

The Short Answer

The short answer: use AI for the scaffolding — topics, drafts, and sections — but write the opening and the personal bit yourself. That's the tested formula. ChatGPT free handles the drafting capably; Rytr at $7.50/mo adds email templates and speeds up short sections; Grammarly catches the typos before send. Subscribers stay for the voice, and the voice is yours.

Why most AI newsletters sound dead

In our 30-day test, we generated a full newsletter with AI and it read like every other AI newsletter: competent, generic, forgettable. The diagnosis wasn't the tool — it was where AI was used. AI drafting the whole thing removes the reason people subscribed to you.

The fix we landed on: AI drafts everything that's structural (the recap, the how-to, the links roundup) and you write the two things that can't be delegated — the opening where you talk to the reader, and the personal story or opinion. Readers who subscribe for your takes stay for the takes.

The weekly workflow (tested over 30 days)

  1. Collect the week. Keep a running note of what happened, what you read, what you used. (10 min/day, this is the raw material.)
  2. Draft with AI. 'Here's my week [paste]. Draft a newsletter: one recap section, one how-to or tutorial, one links roundup, one personal note. Keep each section short.'
  3. Write the voice parts yourself. Replace the AI opening and personal note with your own — this is the 15% of words that carry the relationship.
  4. Tighten with AI. 'Tighten this section without removing facts or changing my voice.'
  5. Proofread. Grammarly or a careful read. Send.

ChatGPT free handled steps 2 and 4 well in every week of our test.

The prompts that worked for us

  • Weekly draft: 'Draft a newsletter from these notes [paste]. Sections: what I did this week, one thing I learned, one tool or link, a short personal note. Under 450 words total. Don't invent events.'
  • Section from a seed: 'Turn this one idea [paste] into a 150-word section that teaches the reader one concrete thing.'
  • Subject line: 'Give me 8 subject line options for a newsletter that [topic]. No clickbait, no exclamation marks.'
  • Voice check: 'Rewrite this paragraph in a more conversational, first-person voice. Keep every fact.'

Keep the voice consistent — the tool that helps

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it okay to use AI to write my newsletter?

Yes — most publishers we tested with use AI for drafting and structure. The formula that kept readers engaged: AI handles the scaffolding (recap, how-to, links), you write the opening and personal note. The voice is what subscribers return for.

Which AI tool is best for newsletters?

For most people, ChatGPT's free tier is enough for drafting. If you publish weekly and want drafts to sound consistently like you, Jasper ($39-49) stores brand voice and kept our test issues consistent. Rytr ($7.50) adds email templates for short sections.

How do I make my AI newsletter not sound like AI?

Write the opening and the personal note yourself, use the AI draft as scaffolding, and keep your specific examples and opinions. In our test, issues with a human opening and personal note held open rates 20-40% better than all-AI issues.

Can AI write good subject lines?

Yes — 'give me 8 subject line options, no clickbait' produced strong options from ChatGPT free in our tests. Test a couple across your audience; the open-rate data will tell you which style your subscribers respond to.
