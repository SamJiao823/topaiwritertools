The short answer: ChatGPT is excellent for the 80% of email that's structural — subject lines, drafts, rewrites, sequences — and poor at the 20% that needs your actual voice and specifics. Use it as a fast assistant, not a ghostwriter. Feed it your context, let it draft, then cut everything generic and keep the details only you could write.

Where ChatGPT genuinely helps

  • Subject lines: ask for 10, pick the least generic. This alone is worth the subscription.
  • First drafts: a newsletter, a cold email, a sequence outline — structured fast, edited later.
  • Rephrasing: 'make this shorter / less salesy / more direct' — the tool is great at variation.
  • Plans: a 5-email welcome sequence outline in 60 seconds, then you fill in the voice.

The workflow that works

Here's the loop we landed on after 30 days of testing:

  1. Give it context first. 'I run a [niche] site. My readers are [audience]. Last email got [result].' The specific context is what separates useful output from generic.
  2. Ask for a draft, not a masterpiece. 'Draft a 120-word newsletter with one point and one link.'
  3. Edit in two passes. First cut every line a robot would write ('I wanted to reach out…'). Then add one specific detail only you know — a number, a name, a result.
  4. Check it against your voice. If you can't imagine saying it out loud, rewrite it. The full edit pass applies to email more than anywhere.

Honest limits

Three places ChatGPT falls short, from our testing:

  • Your voice: it can't know the way you talk unless you show it examples. Paste 3 of your best past emails first — the difference is dramatic.
  • Cold email at scale: a generic AI cold email gets the same zero replies as a generic template. Personalization still has to come from you.
  • Compliance: CAN-SPAM, GDPR, and unsubscribe rules are your responsibility — the AI won't remind you. Always include your physical address and a real unsubscribe link.

Is ChatGPT enough, or pay for a dedicated tool?

If you're already paying for ChatGPT Plus ($20/mo), it can handle most email work. The dedicated tools add structure: Rytr has email templates and a cheaper price ($7.50/mo), and the email-specific tools offer sequence builders. If email is your main content, a dedicated tool's templates save time. If email is occasional, ChatGPT is more than enough.

Bottom line

ChatGPT is a fast, cheap email assistant — not a replacement for your voice. Feed it context, draft fast, cut the robot lines, and add one detail only you know. Do that, and ChatGPT can carry most of your email marketing work.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can ChatGPT write good marketing emails?

Yes for the structure — subject lines, drafts, rewrites, sequences — but not for your voice. Feed it your past emails and add a specific detail to every draft before sending.

Is ChatGPT better than dedicated email tools?

It depends. ChatGPT Plus at $20/mo handles most email work. Dedicated tools add templates and sequence builders — worth it if email is your main content channel.

What's the best ChatGPT prompt for emails?

Give it context first: who you are, who the reader is, what the last email achieved. Then ask for a short draft with one point and one link.

Can I send ChatGPT emails without editing?

You can, but you'll get unsubscribes. Generic AI email is easy to spot. The pass that removes robot lines and adds a personal detail is what gets replies.
