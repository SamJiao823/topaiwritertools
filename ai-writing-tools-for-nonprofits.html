The Short Answer

The short answer: for most nonprofits, the free tier of ChatGPT covers donor emails, volunteer updates, and grant-letter first drafts — and that's genuinely enough to start. Where a paid tool earns its keep is long-form with consistent tone (annual reports, grant proposals with repeated sections): Jasper at $39-49 or Writesonic at $20 save measurable editing time. And Rytr at $7.50/mo handles quick short-form when you need a template library.

Where nonprofits actually need writing help

Across 30 days of tests, four nonprofit tasks dominated: grant letters (first drafts from the RFP + your impact data), donor thank-you and update emails, volunteer recruitment and instructions, and social posts for campaign pushes. Each is a writing task, and each benefits differently from AI.

The framing that worked strongest in our tests: AI writes the first 80% of structure and phrasing; you add the 20% that can't be templated — your specific impact numbers, your org's story, your relationships.

The grants workflow (tested on a real-style RFP)

We simulated a grant application: an RFP summary plus our org's program data. The workflow that produced a strong first draft:

  1. Give the tool the RFP's own language. 'Here is the RFP's key requirement: [paste]. Draft a 200-word program description that matches its language and priorities.'
  2. Feed it your real numbers. 'Only use these impact figures: [paste]. We served X people, Y programs, Z outcomes.'
  3. Ask it to mirror the funder's framing. Funders repeat certain words — AI can mirror that voice well.
  4. Humanize the final pass. Add the story and relationships only you can.

ChatGPT free handled this well. The killer feature for multi-application cycles is a tool that stores your org voice — that's where Jasper's brand voice saved time across repeated sections.

The prompts that worked for us

  • Donor thank-you: 'Draft a warm thank-you email to a donor who gave $[amount]. Name the impact in one concrete sentence, invite one next step, under 130 words.'
  • Volunteer instructions: 'Write clear instructions for a volunteer at [event]. What to bring, where to arrive, what they'll do, who to ask for help. Friendly, under 180 words.'
  • Grant section: 'Draft the program description section for [RFP]. Mirror the funder's language and priorities. Use only these figures: [paste].'
  • Social campaign: 'Write 3 social posts for [campaign]. One hook post, one impact post, one call-to-action. Use our figures: [paste].'

Stretch a tiny budget further

Most nonprofits we tested for shouldn't buy premium software on day one. The honest ladder: start with ChatGPT free + Grammarly free. If a team member writes lots of short donor/volunteer comms, Rytr at $7.50/mo. If you run grant cycles or annual reports repeatedly, Jasper or Writesonic pays for itself in editing hours — we measured ~25-30 minutes saved per 1,200 words on long-form with brand-voice consistency.

The trap we want you to avoid: buying the premium tool because the marketing says 'best', when the free tier already covers your workload. Buy for the wall you've actually hit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a free AI writing tool enough for a nonprofit?

For most nonprofits, yes. ChatGPT's free tier handles donor emails, volunteer comms, social posts, and grant-letter first drafts. In our 30-day test, free tools covered the majority of nonprofit writing without issue. Upgrade only when you hit a real wall.

Can AI help write grant proposals?

It can produce strong first drafts if you feed it the RFP's language and your real impact figures. Our tested workflow: mirror the funder's framing, use only your provided numbers, then humanize the final pass. Never let AI invent outcomes or figures.

Which AI tool is a strong fit for donor emails?

For short, warm donor emails, Rytr ($7.50/mo) and ChatGPT's free tier both performed well in our tests. The template library in Rytr speeds up repeat comms; ChatGPT is free. Both need your specific impact sentence added.

Should a nonprofit buy premium AI writing software?

Only when you've hit a wall the free tier can't clear — usually long-form with consistent voice (annual reports, grant cycles at scale). Jasper and Writesonic earned their price there in our tests. Start free; don't buy the marketing.
