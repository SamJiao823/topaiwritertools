Grammar: Grammarly Wins, but the Gap Shrinks

Grammarly is the category leader for grammar — it catches subtle subject-verb issues, tone drift, and punctuation that a rewriter won’t. QuillBot’s grammar checker is decent and improving, but on long documents Grammarly’s suggestions are more precise. If your problem is “I make mistakes,” Grammarly is the tool.

Rewriting: QuillBot Has No Equal at Its Price

QuillBot’s paraphrasing engine is the standout: flip a sentence through its synonym modes (Standard, Fluency, Formal, etc.) in one click. The free tier caps at 125 words per pass with two modes, which is genuinely useful. Grammarly can rephrase too, but it’s a tone suggestion, not a rewrite engine — a different experience.

Pricing Math

Grammarly Premium: $12/mo on annual billing. QuillBot Premium: $8.33/mo annual or $19.95 monthly. For most people, the honest recommendation is the free tiers first: Grammarly Free for corrections, QuillBot Free for rewrites. Pay only when you hit the caps.

Who Should Pick Which

  • Non-native speakers and anyone fixing grammar → Grammarly.
  • Writers rephrasing existing drafts → QuillBot.
  • Budget-conscious → use both free tiers; they cover most real use.

The Same Sentence, Tested Side by Side

To see how differently they work, we ran one clunky sentence through both: “The meeting was really long and there were a lot of points that were discussed and I think it could have been shorter maybe.”

Grammarly flagged the redundancy, suggested trimming “really” and “a lot of,” tightened the passive construction, and proposed: “The meeting ran long and covered many points; it could have been shorter.” It corrected without changing the sentence’s shape.

QuillBot went further — its paraphrase modes produced a rewritten sentence: “Given the length of the meeting and the number of topics covered, a shorter session might have sufficed.” Same idea, different voice, and you can flip modes until you like the tone.

That one example captures the whole difference: Grammarly polishes your sentence, QuillBot gives you a different one.

For Students and Researchers

Both tools live in the student toolbox, and the honest advice is different per task. QuillBot’s free paraphrasing is a fast way to restate a point in your own words — but academic-integrity rules matter: rephrasing to avoid citation, or “humanizing” AI-written text, is exactly what detectors flag. Grammarly is the safer default for student writing because it improves the text you already wrote instead of replacing it. If you use QuillBot, use it to clarify your sentences, keep citations, and run the result through Grammarly anyway.

The Verdict in One Paragraph

If you misplace commas, overuse “really,” and want your emails to read cleaner, Grammarly is the tool — its free plan covers 90% of that. If you stare at a sentence you can’t fix and want three alternative phrasings in one click, QuillBot is the tool — its free tier covers 125 words a pass, enough for the occasional block. Paying for both is overkill for most people; picking the free tier of whichever matches your bottleneck, then upgrading only when you hit the caps, is the budget move that covers almost everyone.

The Bottom Line

If you make grammar mistakes, Grammarly. If you want to rephrase sentences fast, QuillBot. Most people genuinely benefit from the free tiers of both.
