A veterinary clinic writes a lot of the same text. Vaccination reminders, post-op instructions, recall notices, booster reminders, the welcome paragraph for new puppy owners. Then, occasionally, the worst email of the week: a pet has died and the family needs words.

Safe to automate

  • Vaccination and recall reminders. Date, vaccine, what it protects against, what to do if it lapses. Factual and short.
  • Post-op instruction summaries. Medication, eating, activity limits, the number to call. Generate once, verify once.
  • Practice and service pages. Hours, species treated, emergency policy, new-client welcome.
  • Client education articles. "How to brush a cat's teeth" style content, written from your own protocol.
  • Internal SOPs. Sterilisation checks, intake forms, triage scripts.

Where a vet must stay in the loop

  • Any sentence about a specific animal's condition. The model does not have the chart, the history, or the owner's conversation.
  • Dosage and drug wording. Interactions, species restrictions, off-label use. A plausible sentence here is dangerous.
  • Prognosis and cost estimates. Both change case by case.
  • Easing a bereaved owner. This one never goes to a generator. The right words need the person who met the animal.

What the testing showed

Our 30-day benchmark measured roughly 22 minutes of editing per 1,000 words on the cheap dedicated writer and about 8 minutes on the premium tool — near $7 per piece at $30/hour. A clinic sending hundreds of reminders a month saves real hours; a hospital doing complex case write-ups is buying something else.

Practical setup

Keep a protocol file: your vaccination schedule, post-op rules, emergency policy, common drugs with dosages, and the phrase your clinic uses for after-hours contact. Every prompt cites it, so outputs stay inside your own protocol. Anything touching a named patient's treatment still gets signed off by the vet who saw them.

Do one small thing by hand: the first line of the follow-up email after a serious case. Owners remember the tone of that message for a year.

What vets ask

Can AI write post-op instructions?

Yes from your clinic's protocol. Check dosage, activity limits and contact details yourself — those are the parts that matter.

Is AI safe for vet client emails?

For general reminders, yes. For any statement about an individual animal's treatment, no.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can AI write vaccination reminders for a clinic?

Yes — from your schedule. Dates, vaccine names and intervals stay factual and clinic-specific.

Is it safe to generate post-op instructions?

From your own protocol, then verify. Dosing and activity limits must be checked by a vet.

What about bereavement emails?

Never automated. Those need the vet who treated the animal, in their own words.
