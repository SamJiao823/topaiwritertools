Hypotenuse AI Pricing in 2026: The Full Tier Structure

Two variables drive your bill, and they behave very differently. The billing period is a straight discount: annual billing is cheaper than monthly on every tier where we could verify both numbers. The tier ladder is a step function — the entry plan at $29/mo and the top plan at $230/mo are the same product with different volume ceilings.

Most e-commerce sellers buy too much tier and the wrong billing period. If your output is modest, the tier decision is the one that costs you money; if your output is large, the billing period is.

There Is No Permanent Free Plan — Only a Trial

This is where Hypotenuse differs most from its neighbours. Tools such as Rytr keep an ongoing free tier (10,000 characters a month) that lets you work indefinitely at low volume. Hypotenuse does not — access is time-boxed, and when the trial ends you either subscribe or lose the workspace.

Hypotenuse has listed a 7-day trial, but we could not independently verify the trial length or the word allowance attached to it as of September 2026. If the window really is seven days, use it deliberately: run your real product data through it in the first two days rather than spending the week on the interface tour. The trial is the only zero-cost window you get, and the only sensible time to test whether the workflow fits before you choose a billing period.

Why this changes the buying decision

With no free tier, there is no "use it until I outgrow it" path. Every month of access is a paid month, so the entry tier's suitability matters more than the entry tier's price.

Why Annual Billing Is Where the Discount Lives

Hypotenuse's monthly and annual prices are far enough apart that the billing period is effectively a second pricing decision sitting on top of the tier choice.

  • Entry tier: $29/mo monthly versus about $19/mo annually — roughly 34% less.
  • Essential: $87/mo monthly versus about $56/mo annually — roughly 36% less.
  • Twelve-month totals at the entry tier: about $348 paying monthly versus about $228 paying annually, a difference of about $120 in the first year.

That $120 is larger than the gap between several competing tools' entry plans, so it is not a rounding error. The trade-off is commitment: annual billing turns a flexible monthly cost into a sunk one. The sequencing that avoids that risk is obvious but frequently skipped — run the trial first, then pick the billing period with trial data in hand.

Word Limits and Overage Charges

Hypotenuse's caps are where the pricing gets genuinely uncertain, and we are going to state the uncertainty rather than resolve it. Sources conflict on the entry tier's monthly allowance:

  • Some sources list 25,000 words per month on the entry tier.
  • Others list 50,000 words per month on the same tier.
  • We could not verify which figure is current as of September 2026.

That is a factor-of-two disagreement on the single number that decides whether the entry tier is good value. Treat any confident claim about Hypotenuse's word allowance — including ours — with suspicion until the vendor confirms it in writing.

Exceeding the cap is the other half of the picture. Based on the sources we reviewed, going over your monthly allowance produces overage charges rather than a hard stop, so the bill can grow past the sticker price. We could not verify the per-word overage rate as of September 2026. Combined with the disputed cap, that makes overage the least predictable line on a Hypotenuse invoice — you cannot calculate your worst-case month without a number the public sources do not agree on.

The Cost the Pricing Table Does Not Show: Your Editing Time

Subscription price is the visible cost of an AI writing tool. Editing time is the larger one, and it is the reason a cheaper tier is not automatically the cheaper tool. In our 30-day blind benchmark across eight tools, Hypotenuse averaged 14 minutes of editing per 1,500-word piece, against 8 minutes for Claude and Jasper and 10 minutes for ChatGPT. We do not adjust that number — it is the measured result.

Put a rate on it and the gap becomes concrete. At an assumed $40 per hour of your time, 14 minutes of editing costs about $9.33 per piece against about $5.33 for 8 minutes — roughly $4 more per piece. Publish 20 pieces a month and that is about $80 of extra editing labour, more than the entry-tier subscription itself.

The pricing lesson

Hypotenuse's subscription at about $19/mo on annual billing is not the number that decides whether it is worth it. Total cost is subscription plus editing time, and editing time is the bigger line for most users.

How to Decide in Five Minutes

  1. Start the trial and use your real catalog. Feed it your own product data in the first two days rather than evaluating templates with sample text.
  2. Count the words you generated. That number, not the feature list, picks your row in the volume table above.
  3. Ask Hypotenuse for the current entry-tier cap in writing. The 25,000 versus 50,000 disagreement is only resolvable by the vendor, and it changes the answer.
  4. Ask for the overage rate at the same time. If the cap is disputed, your worst-case month depends on a number that is not published.
