What Do AI Writing Tools Really Cost? The $0–$49 Breakdown (2026)

Sticker prices are only half the story. After 30 days of testing, the tools that look cheap can cost you the most in editing time — here is the real monthly cost, with the formula so you can run the numbers yourself.

By Sam Jiao · 30-day tested · Benchmark data updated September 15, 2026

Disclosure: We may earn a commission when you purchase through links. See our affiliate disclosure.

The short answer: Sticker prices run from $0 (ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini free tiers) to $79/mo annual (Writesonic, now a GEO platform; about $99/mo if billed monthly). But the real monthly cost includes editing time — and that flips the math. In our 30-day benchmark we timed editing on every piece, and the tool with the lowest sticker price in the set finished second-highest in real monthly cost. A $7.50 subscription is not a $7.50 tool if it costs you eleven dollars of editing per piece.

Naming note: Grammarly renamed its paid individual tier from Premium to Pro. Older articles — including some of ours — still use "Premium" for the same plan.

Three parts follow: the verified price ladder, the editing-time formula with the actual numbers, and a table that puts both together. Every figure is either a price we re-checked in September 2026 or arithmetic you can reproduce on a calculator; assumptions are labelled as assumptions.

The formula, so you can re-run it

The arithmetic is deliberately simple. One formula per piece, one for the month:

editing cost per piece = editing minutes ÷ 60 × hourly rate monthly editing cost = editing cost per piece × pieces per month real monthly cost = sticker price + monthly editing cost

Worked example for Rytr, which is the tool this calculation flatters least:

22 min ÷ 60 = 0.3667 hours 0.3667 × $30/hr = $11.00 per piece $11.00 × 10 pieces = $110.00 per month in editing $110.00 + $7.50 sticker = $117.50 real monthly cost

And the same three steps for Jasper, the tool it flatters most:

8 min ÷ 60 = 0.1333 hours 0.1333 × $30/hr = $4.00 per piece $4.00 × 10 pieces = $40.00 per month in editing $40.00 + $49.00 sticker = $89.00 real monthly cost
Two assumptions, stated plainly. First, the hourly rate is an assumed $30/hour blended rate — it is a modelling input, not something we measured. If your time is worth $15/hour the editing column halves; at $60/hour it doubles. Second, the monthly volume is an assumed 10 finished pieces per month — also a modelling input, not a measurement. Your own volume changes the monthly column proportionally, and the per-piece figures above let you substitute your own numbers directly.

What the assumptions buy you is an internally consistent comparison — the same rate and volume applied to every tool — which is enough to see which tools pay for themselves and which quietly cost more than they appear to.

The sharpest comparison in the dataset is Rytr versus Jasper: $7.00 per piece in extra editing, or $70 a month at 10 pieces — more than the $41.50 sticker gap between them. That is not an argument that Jasper is cheap. It is an argument that the sticker price is the wrong number to sort by.

Tool-by-tool: what you actually pay

Claude — $0–20/mo, ~8 min editing

The best value line on this page. Claude scored 9/10 on both short-form and long-form, the top result in the set, with the joint-fastest ~8 minutes of editing. That is $40/month of real cost on the free tier at our assumed rate, $60 on Pro. What you do not get at any price is SEO tooling. See Claude pricing.

ChatGPT — $0–200/mo, ~10 min editing

Scored 8/10 in both categories with ~10 minutes of editing — $50/month real cost on the free tier. The paid ladder above it is long, and the honest read is that most writers do not need past Plus. ChatGPT Team is the one line that scales with headcount rather than usage.

Rytr — $7.50/mo (annual), ~22 min editing

The most instructive tool on this page. At $7.50 it is the cheapest paid tier anywhere in our test, and on short-form it earned a respectable 7/10. But ~22 minutes of editing per piece is the longest in the set, and at our assumed rate that is $110/month of editing — which makes it the second-most-expensive tool here in real terms despite having the lowest sticker price. If your volume is low and your pieces are short, the editing bill never arrives. If you are producing daily, it does. More detail in our Rytr review and the free plan breakdown.

Jasper — $39–69/mo, ~8 min editing

$49 monthly or $39 annual for Creator, with the cheapest editing time in the set at ~8 minutes. That produces an $89/month real cost — high in absolute terms, lower than both Rytr and Copy.ai, and the gap widens with volume. You are paying for brand-voice consistency and team workflow, not a quality jump: 9/10 short-form against Rytr's 7/10 is real, but not a 6.5× one.

Copy.ai — From $29/mo, ~15 min editing

No free tier, so real monthly cost starts at $104. It scored 7/10 short-form and 6/10 long-form with ~15 minutes of editing. The value here is go-to-market workflow rather than prose — a workflow software price with editing time attached. See Copy.ai pricing.

Hypotenuse — $19–29/mo, ~14 min editing

$19 annual or $29 monthly, ~14 minutes of editing, 7/10 short-form and 6/10 long-form. Real monthly cost lands at $89. The economics only work at catalogue scale where bulk product descriptions are the whole job. See Hypotenuse pricing.

Grammarly — ~$12–30/mo, no editing-time column

The one tool here priced as a second subscription rather than a first. At ~$12/month annual it is the cheapest paid line in the set, and the case is specific: sentence-level tone and fluency, particularly for non-native English writers. It does not generate, so it has no editing-time figure — and while it is the only tool here that could plausibly reduce another tool's editing column, our dataset does not measure that. We are not going to guess at it.

Writesonic — from $79/mo, ~12 min editing

The most expensive line in the benchmark. $79/month plus $60 of editing is $139 real monthly cost. What justifies it is native SEO tooling and GEO (AI-search visibility) tracking, which nothing else here has built in. If AI-search visibility is your explicit goal that is a product you can buy; if it is not, you are paying a premium for prose that scored no better than a free tier.

The percentages are the monthly price minus the annual price, divided by the monthly price — Grammarly's is 18 ÷ 30 = 60%, Jasper's is 10 ÷ 49 = 20.4%, Copy.ai's is 5 ÷ 29 = 17.2%, and Hypotenuse's is 10 ÷ 29 = 34%.

Two things to weigh. Size first: Grammarly's $216 annual saving is larger than the entire annual cost of most other tools here, which makes annual billing the obvious choice there if you have decided to buy at all. Risk second: an annual commitment locks you in for twelve months while model quality and pricing both move faster than that. For a first purchase, a month of monthly billing is cheap insurance; take the discount on renewal, once you know you are staying.

Annual billing also does not change editing time by a single minute — it shrinks the sticker column and leaves the larger column untouched. Jasper's $120 annual saving is real, but smaller than the $840 of annual editing cost its $7-per-piece advantage over Rytr represents at 10 pieces a month.

Which tier fits which scenario

Rather than a blanket answer, here is the scenario-based math from our tests:

  • Student / light use: the $0 stack — a free tier plus Grammarly's free plan. Real cost is your editing time and nothing else. See best free tools.
  • Solo freelancer, short-form volume: $7.50 (Rytr) or a free tier — not both, and not a third.
  • Marketer publishing SEO content: ~$20 (ChatGPT Plus) for plain SEO writing, or Writesonic from $79/mo only if AI-search visibility is the explicit goal — the one scenario where that line is defensible.
  • Team with brand-voice needs: $39–49 (Jasper). Consistency is what you are buying, and it is the only thing here that gets cheaper per piece as volume rises.
  • Long-form professional: $20 (Claude Pro) for coherence, or Jasper if brand voice matters too.
  • E-commerce catalogue: $19–29 (Hypotenuse), and only for bulk product copy. Below that, the editing bill exceeds the subscription.

The mistake that costs the most

The biggest money waster is not picking the wrong tool — it is subscribing to two tools that do the same job. In our test, running ChatGPT Plus and Rytr and Writesonic for the same writing tasks meant $106.50/month for work one tool could do: $20 + $7.50 + $79.

The discipline that keeps costs sane: one drafting tool plus one polish tool (Grammarly) at most. Everything past that is a workflow feature you have not justified yet. If you are weighing a specific pair, see Rytr vs Jasper or cheap AI writing tools.

Bottom line

The honest monthly cost of AI writing in 2026 is $0–$79 in sticker price, and roughly $40–$139 once editing time is priced in at our assumed $30/hour across an assumed 10 pieces a month. Editing time is what reorders the ladder: it turns the cheapest subscription in our test into the second-most-expensive tool in real terms.

The practical version: start on a free tier, add exactly one paid tool when volume or workflow demands it, and re-run the formula with your own hourly rate before committing to annual billing. It is three lines. It takes less time than reading another pricing page.
