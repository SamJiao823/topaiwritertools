Social Media Caption Templates: 7 Structures, and the One Line to Cut From Each

Most captions fail in the same place: the first line asks for attention without giving a reason. Over thirty days we tested seven structures across product posts, tips and announcements, and kept the ones that earned saves rather than likes.

Likes are cheap and saves are the signal that matters. A caption someone saves is a caption they intend to come back to, which usually means it taught something or listed something. All seven structures below are built around that: give the reason to save inside the first two lines, not after the third paragraph.

What every structure has in common

  • The hook is a claim, not a greeting. "New drop" is a greeting. "Four ways to do X without Y" is a claim.
  • Line breaks every one or two sentences. A wall of text is scrolled past regardless of what it says.
  • The ask is specific and late. "Save this for your next launch" beats "follow for more".
  • One caption, one idea. Two ideas in one caption means neither is remembered.

1. The number list

[NUMBER] [things] that [outcome] — without [the common cost].

1. [SHORT, SPECIFIC]
2. [SHORT, SPECIFIC]
3. [SHORT, SPECIFIC]

[ONE LINE: who this is for and when they will need it.]

Save this for [the moment it becomes useful].

Cut this line: the closing "what do you think?" — it asks for a comment nobody has a reason to write.

2. The mistake

[THE MISTAKE] is why [the bad outcome].

Here is what actually happens: [ONE OR TWO SENTENCES, PLAIN].

Do this instead: [THE FIX, ONE STEP].

I did this wrong for [TIME PERIOD] before [WHAT CHANGED].

Cut this line: any version of "we have all been there". It pads and it says nothing.

3. The before and after

Before: [CONCRETE STATE — a number, a time, a thing you had].
After: [CONCRETE STATE].

The only thing that changed: [ONE CHANGE].

[ONE HONEST LINE ABOUT WHAT DID NOT IMPROVE.]

Cut this line: the superlative. If the after state needs an adjective to sound good, the numbers were not specific enough.

4. The process

How [OUTCOME] actually gets done, in [NUMBER] steps.

Step 1: [VERB, OBJECT, TIME IF KNOWN]
Step 2: [VERB, OBJECT]
Step 3: [VERB, OBJECT]

The step people skip: [WHICH ONE, AND WHAT IT COSTS].

Cut this line: "it is easier than you think". If it were, the post would not be needed.

5. The quiet announcement

[WHAT IT IS] is live.

It does [ONE THING]. It does not do [THE THING PEOPLE WILL ASSUME].

[WHO IT IS FOR, IN ONE LINE].

[WHERE TO FIND IT, PLAINLY].

Cut this line: "we are excited to announce". Three words of nothing at the top of the only three lines people see before tapping more.

6. The behind-the-scenes

[THE UNGLAMOROUS DETAIL] took [TIME / NUMBER OF ATTEMPTS].

Here is the part nobody posts: [ONE CONCRETE DETAIL].

[ONE SENTENCE ON WHY IT MATTERED, OR WHY IT DID NOT.]

Cut this line: the journey metaphor. "It has been quite the journey" appears in almost every generated version of this post.

7. The question that answers itself

[QUESTION YOUR AUDIENCE ACTUALLY ASKS].

Short answer: [ONE SENTENCE].

Longer answer, because the short one is wrong sometimes: [TWO OR THREE SENTENCES WITH THE CONDITION].

Save this — the condition is the part people forget.

Cut this line: "drop your questions below" when you have already answered the only question the post raises.

Why generated captions get scrolled

A model writes a competent caption in the register of a brand, not of a person. The result is grammatical, complete, and has no reason for anyone to stop. The fix is not a better prompt; it is supplying the one thing only you have — the number, the attempt count, the thing that did not work. Every structure above has a slot for exactly that, and the caption only works when that slot is filled with something real.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do caption templates hurt engagement?

The structure does not; the filler does. A template gives you the order of the lines, and the specific detail you put in the bracket is what makes it worth saving.

What should the first line do?

Make a claim rather than a greeting. "Four ways to do X without Y" gives a reason to read; "new drop" does not.

Why optimise for saves instead of likes?

A save means the reader intends to return to it, which happens when the post teaches or lists something. Likes cost the reader nothing and signal less.

How many hashtags should a caption have?

Fewer than most guides suggest, and placed after the ask rather than inside the hook. The hook is the only part most readers see.
