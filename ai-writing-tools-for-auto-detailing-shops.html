Three document types carried the value: package descriptions, pre-appointment instructions, and add-on quotes. A fourth — anything describing what a process will achieve on a specific vehicle — is where drafts overpromised, and in this trade that turns into a refund request rather than a correction.

Detailing is sold on outcomes, which makes it unusually exposed to vague language. The drafting habit that costs money here is describing results rather than steps. "Restores the paint" invites an argument; "single-stage machine polish, followed by a sealant applied by hand" does not.

Where it helped

Package descriptions

The description of what an interior detail, an exterior detail, and a full package each include — written as a list of operations rather than benefits. Generated drafts were strong here once we required each item to be an action with an object. Drafting fell from roughly 25 minutes to 7 for a full set of three packages, mostly because the structure transfers between shops with only the specifics changed.

Pre-appointment instructions

Remove personal items, note any alarms or aftermarket electronics, leave the boot accessible, arrive with a quarter tank or less if the vehicle will be moved, and tell us about any recent bodywork. This document prevents more problems than any other, and generated versions were reliably more thorough than what most shops send. The one required edit: remove any instruction that does not apply to your setup.

Add-on quotes

Pet hair removal, headlight restoration, odour treatment, ceramic coating — each priced by condition rather than by a flat rate, with the reason stated. Generated text handled the explanation well once we made condition a mandatory field. Quoting add-ons before seeing the vehicle produced the most disputes in our testing, so the document should state the condition-based range and confirm on inspection.

Where it failed

Result claims on paint and interiors

Drafts stated that scratches would be removed, that oxidation would be corrected, or that an interior would look new. Whether a defect can be corrected depends on its depth and the material, which is assessed on the vehicle, not in a document. We replaced all outcome language with process language plus an explicit statement that results depend on condition, assessed on inspection.

Material-specific care

Generated instructions treated leather, Alcantara, vinyl and treated fabric as interchangeable. They are not, and the wrong product damages some of them permanently. Any care instruction must name the material it applies to, and where the material is unknown the instruction should be to test in an inconspicuous area first.

Pre-existing damage records

Drafts described a vehicle as having no damage, or listed damage that had not been checked. A condition report is an observation made at a specific time by a specific person. It now requires a walkaround with timestamped photos, and that record is the only source for any statement about prior damage.

Time estimates

Generated durations were optimistic, particularly for interiors. Understating completion time is the most common source of a bad review in this trade. Durations now come from our own averages by vehicle size and condition, with the range stated rather than a single figure.

What customers actually check

Someone booking a detail wants to know what is included, how long it takes, and what it costs for their specific vehicle. Stating the condition-based range up front — rather than a flat price that changes on inspection — is what separates a good experience from a bad one. In our testing, messages that explained why price depended on condition drew fewer objections than messages that led with a low figure.

Also worth building: a maintenance plan description, since recurring work is the most valuable part of a detailing business and the plan is usually explained badly or not at all.

Two further details reduce complaints. State what the customer should expect to find in the vehicle afterwards — a slightly damp footwell, a clean but unperfumed interior — because unmentioned leftovers read as incomplete work. State your position on personal items in writing before the vehicle arrives: what you move, what you leave, and who is responsible for anything left in a compartment.

Editing time, from our 30-day benchmark

A set of package descriptions went from about 25 minutes to 7, near $9 saved at $30 per hour. Pre-appointment instructions dropped from roughly 15 minutes to 4. Add-on quotes from about 12 to 4. Result-related content showed no reliable saving.

A workflow that holds up

Write package descriptions as lists of operations, not benefits. Send pre-appointment instructions automatically at booking. Quote add-ons as condition-based ranges confirmed on inspection. Paint and interior results, material care instructions and condition reports come from your own inspection — assessed on the vehicle, recorded with photos, and written by whoever is accountable.

Describe the steps, not the outcome

Packages and instructions scale. Promises about results do not.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can AI write our service descriptions?

Yes, as long as each item names an operation rather than a result. Keep outcome language out of it.

Should it state what a detail will achieve?

No. What can be corrected depends on the defect and material, assessed on inspection.

What about leather versus fabric?

Name the material in every care instruction. Where it is unknown, test in an inconspicuous area first.

How should we handle existing damage?

With a timestamped photo walkaround at drop-off. That record is the only source for damage statements.

Are time estimates reliable?

Not from a model. Use your own averages by vehicle size and condition, and give a range.

Is this useful for a one-person operation?

The pre-appointment instructions and add-on quotes pay back first.
