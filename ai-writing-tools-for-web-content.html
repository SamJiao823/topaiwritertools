The short answer: AI is a reasonable fit for routine web content — service pages, location pages, FAQ blocks, category descriptions — because these follow patterns and there are usually many of them. It is a poor fit for the small number of pages that carry your actual differentiation, since those are precisely the pages where generic writing is most damaging.

The uniformity trap

The failure mode on twenty service pages is not that any single one is bad, it is that all twenty say the same thing. Search engines treat near-identical pages as low value, and visitors notice too. Three habits prevent it:

  • Give each page a distinct job. Ten pages targeting one intent compete with each other rather than compounding.
  • Feed real page-specific input. Actual scope, actual pricing logic, actual constraints — generic briefs produce generic output.
  • Vary the structure, not just the wording. Different opening angle, different proof type, different objection handled.

Start from what you already know

The best raw material is already in your business: proposal documents, onboarding emails, support replies, and the questions customers ask before buying. Paste that into the prompt rather than asking the model to invent content from a page title. This is the single change that most improves output quality, and it also keeps the facts correct.

Keep the handful of important pages human-written

Your homepage positioning, your pricing narrative and your flagship service page are where a visitor decides whether you are a real business or a template. These are small in number and worth writing personally. Everything routine around them can be assisted.

A working process

  1. Decide the page's single search intent before writing — one page, one job.
  2. Paste real material (proposals, tickets, FAQs) as input rather than starting from nothing.
  3. Generate structure first, not prose; approve the outline, then write sections.
  4. Verify every factual sentence — pricing, scope, guarantees, certifications.
  5. Read adjacent pages side by side to catch the copy starting to converge.

What do people ask about AI writing tools for web content?

Can AI write website content?

It suits routine pages with repeatable structure — service pages, categories, FAQs — particularly at volume. Pages that carry your positioning and differentiation should be written by you, since those are where generic copy does the most harm.

Why do my AI-written pages all sound the same?

Because they were generated from similar briefs. Give each page a distinct intent, feed genuine page-specific details, and vary the structure rather than only rewording the sentences.

What input produces the best web copy?

Material you already own: proposals, onboarding emails, support replies and pre-sale questions. Real input keeps output both specific and factually correct.

Which pages should I write myself?

Your homepage positioning, pricing narrative and flagship service page. They are few in number and carry the decision about whether you look like a real business.
