First, every figure below carries its billing period, because Copy.ai's annual discount is not trivia - it is $60 a year on the entry plan alone. Second, we separate what Copy.ai publishes from what we could actually confirm in our own 30 days of testing. Where we could not confirm something, the page says so instead of smoothing it over.

The most recent sources we found, dated September 2026, state that Copy.ai has retired the free plan for new sign-ups and now keeps only standalone free tools - individual utilities you can use without a subscription, rather than a free workspace with a monthly word allowance. If you are evaluating Copy.ai in 2026, assume the practical entry point is the paid Chat plan at $29/mo, and treat any surviving free access as a trial rather than a permanent tier. Budget for the subscription; if a free tier turns out to be available when you sign up, that is a bonus, not a plan.

Chat: $29/mo monthly or $24/mo annual - what the $60 discount costs you

Chat is Copy.ai's writing plan, and it is the only tier on the list an individual writer would buy without a finance conversation. The arithmetic is simple enough to do here:

  • Monthly billing: $29 per month, cancel any time, $348 over twelve months.
  • Annual billing: $24 per month, charged as roughly $288 for the year, saving $60 against monthly.

That is a 17% discount - the monthly price minus the annual price, divided by the monthly price, or 5 / 29. It is a real saving but a modest one, and it comes with the usual annual-billing risk: you are locked in for twelve months while both model quality and competitor pricing move faster than that. For a first purchase we would pay monthly for one or two months, confirm the tool earns its place in your week, and take the annual rate on renewal.

What Chat includes at either price: unlimited words inside the Chat interface, brand voice, access to the template library, and up to 5 seats in one workspace. That seat allowance is the most underrated line in the whole plan. Five seats at $24/mo annual works out to under $5 per person per month, which is genuinely cheap for a team - as long as the whole team is writing inside Copy.ai's chat rather than running automated workflows. That distinction is the whole story of the next section.

The $29-to-$1,000 gap: what actually changes at the Growth tier

A 34x price step between adjacent plans is unusual, and it is worth understanding what it represents, because it explains why so many people try Copy.ai and bounce off it.

Chat is priced like writing software. It competes, on price, with ChatGPT Plus at $20/mo and Rytr at $7.50/mo on annual billing - a crowded, cheap market. Growth is priced like GTM (go-to-market) infrastructure: it is sold on workflow volume, integrations, and priority support, with automation that chains research, drafting and publishing steps into a repeatable pipeline. At roughly $1,000/mo and up, you are no longer shopping for a writing tool. You are buying a piece of your revenue operations stack, and it is being priced against sales and marketing software rather than against word processors.

The practical consequence is the one thing we would want a buyer to take away from this page: the automation capability people associate with Copy.ai is what pushes the price into four figures, and it is not available on the $29 tier. If what you actually need is prose, you are looking at the wrong plan family entirely. If what you need is a repeatable pipeline, the entry writing plan will not give it to you, and the honest comparison is not "Copy.ai vs ChatGPT" but "Copy.ai Growth vs building the same pipeline out of cheaper parts."

One more warning: buyers frequently upgrade to Growth expecting a better writing product and find the same prose with more workflow capacity. The text generation does not change tier to tier in any way we could measure. What changes is how much of it you can automate and how much support you get when it breaks.

What you are really buying: workflow automation, not prose

Copy.ai's real differentiator is chaining steps together - research into drafting into publishing - and in our 30-day blind benchmark that genuinely helped teams producing structured content on repeat. The trade-off showed up in the editing numbers.

Across the eight tools in our 30-day test, measured per finished piece at roughly 1,500 words, Copy.ai recorded the second-longest editing time in the set at about 15 minutes per piece. For comparison: Claude and Jasper were the fastest at about 8 minutes per piece, ChatGPT needed about 10, Writesonic about 12, and Hypotenuse about 14. Only Rytr was slower, at about 22 minutes. Copy.ai scored 7/10 on short-form and 6/10 on long-form in the same test. The full dataset, including how the scores were produced and what the study's limits are, is in our 2026 benchmark; the method itself is on how we test.

Read those numbers carefully, because they cut against the price. You are paying a workflow-software premium for prose that takes longer to clean up than ChatGPT's, at $29/mo monthly against ChatGPT Plus at $20/mo - 45% more for a slower draft. The automation is not a nice extra on top of the writing; it is the entire reason the product exists on this page. If your process is "one person, one draft, one publish," the workflow layer has nothing to automate and the premium buys you nothing measurable.

Who should pay for Copy.ai - and who should not

The scenario math, from our own testing rather than from the marketing pages:

  • GTM or marketing team running repeatable content operations: the only clear yes, and realistically the only case that justifies Growth. The automation is real and it saves hours at volume. Go in expecting to negotiate on price.
  • Small team that just wants shared seats and unlimited words: Chat at $24/mo annual is defensible at 5 seats, roughly $5 per person. You are buying a shared workspace, not automation.
  • Solo writer or freelancer: skip it. Rytr at $7.50/mo annual or ChatGPT Plus at $20/mo gives you more usable output per dollar, and neither charges a workflow premium you cannot use.
  • Occasional writer: the math is worst here. If the free tier really is gone, light use now carries the $29/mo entry cost, and at three or four pieces a month that is expensive per piece. Use a free tier of something else until the volume justifies a subscription - see free AI writing tools.
