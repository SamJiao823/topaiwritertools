What coaches and consultants actually write

The writing load is surprisingly heavy: lead magnets, workshop outlines, course syllabi, sales pages, client follow-ups, and social proof posts. It's all trust-building, and it's all repetitive enough that AI pays for itself fast. Our 30-day test focused on the two biggest time sinks: lead magnets and course outlines.

Lead magnets: the free-to-paid bridge

A lead magnet is short, structured, and low-stakes — an ideal AI task. We generated checklists, mini-guides, and email sequences from one prompt pattern: 'You are a [niche] coach. Write a 10-point checklist called [title] for [audience] solving [specific problem]. One concrete tip per point, actionable in under 10 minutes.'

Every tool we tested produced a usable first draft. The edit that mattered was replacing generic tips with the coach's actual method — which is the whole point of the lead magnet, so budget 30 minutes there. Our full workflow on this lives in AI for ebooks and lead magnets.

Course outlines and syllabi

Outlines are where AI genuinely shines. A structured request — 'Design a 6-week course on [topic]. Each week: one outcome, 3 lessons, one exercise, one success metric' — produces a defensible skeleton in seconds. The model's biggest value: it forces you to make the curriculum decisions explicit, which is the part coaches procrastinate on.

Sales pages and proposals

This is where the model matters more. A sales page has to sound like you, and generic AI copy kills credibility for a solo coach. In testing, Claude Pro produced the most natural, least 'AI-sounding' long-form of the tools we tried — which is exactly why coaches tend to land there. ChatGPT's free tier can draft the structure; Claude is the upgrade when voice consistency starts mattering. See also how to write landing pages with AI.

Client communication

Client emails, progress notes, and follow-ups are short-form, and our data says short-form is where cheap tools hold up. Rytr at $7.50 handled these about 90% as well as Jasper at $39. The risk isn't quality — it's sounding canned. Two rules from testing: always insert one client-specific detail the model doesn't know, and keep the tone informal.

The honest money question

Is a paid tool worth it for a solo coach? Our verdict from the 30-day test: usually no, until you need brand-voice consistency across many pieces. ChatGPT free tier plus a strong editing habit covers lead magnets and outlines. Claude at $20 is the first upgrade that's clearly worth it. Frequently Asked Questions

Can a coach use free AI tools, or do they need a paid one?

Start free. ChatGPT's free tier handles lead magnets, outlines, and client emails. The first paid upgrade that's clearly worth it in our 30-day test was Claude Pro at $20 — it produces the most natural long-form voice for sales pages and proposals.

How do I choose an AI tool for writing course outlines?

Any capable model produces a solid outline from a structured prompt. We test the tools in our course-creator guide. The outline is only as good as the structure you ask for — outcome, lessons, exercise, metric per module.

How do I keep AI content from sounding like AI?

Feed it a sample of your own writing as a style reference, specify the tone, and rewrite the first paragraph yourself. In testing, Claude Pro produced the most natural voice, but the style reference matters more than the tool.

Is it okay to sell an AI-drafted lead magnet?

Yes — it's a drafting tool. The value you add is your method, which the model can't fake. Budget your time for replacing generic tips with your real process; that's what makes the lead magnet convert.
